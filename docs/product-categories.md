# Product category assessment — what each product legally *is*

**Status: incomplete, and deliberately so.** This is the preparation for a
decision that needs a regulatory lawyer in Sweden and Germany. It sets out
what determines the answer, what our products look like against those
criteria, and which ones are uncomfortable. It is not legal advice, and
none of these categories should be treated as settled until someone
qualified signs them off.

Sister document: [claims-policy.md](claims-policy.md) — how we may speak
once a category is settled.

---

## Why this comes first

Every other compliance question follows from the category. Category sets
the permitted claims, the labelling, the registration, who must be
notified, and whether the product may be sold at all in a given market.

Getting it wrong upwards (selling an unlicensed medicine) is the serious
one. In Sweden that is Läkemedelsverket's territory; in Germany,
BVL/BfArM and the Heilmittelwerbegesetz.

---

## The categories in play

| Category | What makes it this | What you may say | Who oversees (SE) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Food** | An ordinary foodstuff | Taste, tradition, ingredients. No health claims beyond authorised ones | Livsmedelsverket |
| **Food supplement** | Concentrated nutrients/botanicals, in a dose form, to supplement the diet | Authorised nutrition/health claims only. Botanical claims are on hold → effectively none | Livsmedelsverket + local authority notification |
| **Botanical preparation** | Not a separate legal box — sits inside food supplement or medicine depending on presentation and pharmacology | — | — |
| **Cosmetic** | Applied externally, for cleansing/appearance | Appearance claims only. Requires CPNP notification, safety assessment, responsible person | Läkemedelsverket |
| **Traditional herbal medicinal product (THMP)** | Herbal, for minor conditions, 30 years of traditional use (15 in EU). Registered, not authorised | May state traditional therapeutic indications | Läkemedelsverket |
| **Medicinal product** | By presentation *or* by pharmacological function | Only what the licence says | Läkemedelsverket |

Two traps:

- **"Supplement" is not a self-declaration.** Calling a 60% ethanol
  tincture a supplement does not make it one. Dose form, alcohol content,
  the pharmacology of the actives and the presentation all feed the
  decision, and the authority's view wins.
- **Alcohol content matters separately.** High-ethanol drops may hit
  alcohol rules and labelling duties independently of the food/medicine
  question.

## What determines the answer for us

Fill this in per product, per market:

1. **Composition** — every species, part used, extraction ratio, solvent,
   final concentration of the actives.
2. **Is any species on a restricted or medicinal-only list** in that
   market? (Amanita muscaria is the live example: muscimol is a scheduled
   narcotic in Sweden, while the mushroom is legal in Germany and most of
   the EU.)
3. **Dose form and posology** — drops with a stated dose look medicinal;
   a culinary bitters bottle looks like food.
4. **Pharmacological activity at the intended dose** — the "by function"
   test. A dose with a real pharmacological effect points to medicine
   however it is sold.
5. **Presentation** — name, label, page copy, MYCO, testimonials.
6. **Market** — Sweden and Germany can and do answer differently for the
   same bottle.

## What is needed for each product

A file per product, holding:

- full species list with Latin names, parts used, extraction ratio, solvent
- final alcohol content
- intended daily amount and the reasoning behind it
- all species checked against the Swedish and German restricted lists
- the claims used on the page, checked against this category
- supplier documentation and certificates of analysis
- for Shilajit: heavy-metal testing per batch
- label copy in the required languages

## Labelling — what a food supplement must carry (EU)

Missing labels are the most common and most avoidable breach:

- the words "food supplement"
- the species, part used and amount per daily portion
- the recommended daily amount and a warning not to exceed it
- "do not use as a substitute for a varied diet"
- "keep out of reach of young children"
- allergens, alcohol content, net quantity, batch, best-before
- the name and address of the food business operator — **that is you, and
  it needs a registered business identity behind it**

## Before selling in a new market

Notify the relevant authority where required (in Sweden, the local
municipality registers the food business; several member states require a
per-product notification before first sale). Check species lists per
market — they differ.

1. **Decide "ADHD Support"** — rename or withdraw. It is the one item
   that could colour a regulator's reading of the entire range.
2. **Decide Amanita by market**, and enforce it at checkout — no shipping
   to Sweden until you have written advice.
3. **Write the composition file** for the ten extract products.
4. **Take one hour of regulatory advice** with those files in hand, in
   Sweden first, then Germany. One session with the papers ready is worth
   more than months of guessing.

6. Only then expand the range.

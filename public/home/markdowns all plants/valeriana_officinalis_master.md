## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Valeriana officinalis L. — Native to Europe and Asia; widely cultivated in temperate regions. Perennial herb up to ~2 m; rhizome and roots harvested in autumn of the second year for highest concentration of valerenic acids and volatile oils.[web:6][web:13][web:21]

**Botanical Family:**  
Caprifoliaceae (formerly Valerianaceae).[web:21]

**Part Used:**  
Valerianae radix — Dried rhizome with roots (most potent; valerenic acids and volatile oils concentrated in root tissue).[web:13]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Rhizomes dug in autumn after aerial parts have died back; washed, sliced if large, and dried at low temperature (<40°C) to protect valepotriates and volatile oils; stored airtight and protected from light and heat to limit oxidation.[web:13]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Dark brown to yellowish rhizome with numerous fibrous roots; strong, characteristic earthy/pungent odor (due to bornyl isovalerate and other volatiles); slightly bitter taste; absence of mold and excessive woody material.[web:21]

**Storage:**  
Airtight containers in a cool, dark, dry location. Optimal potency up to 2–3 years; volatile fractions slowly diminish over time.[web:21]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**  

**Primary Bioactives (Valerenic Acids & Related):**  
- **Valerenic acid, hydroxyvalerenic acid, acetoxyvalerenic acid:** Positive modulation of GABA-A receptors, inhibition of GABA breakdown, and mild adenosine/serotonin modulation; sedative and anxiolytic actions.[web:21]  

**Supporting Compounds:**  
- **Volatile oils (0.2–1%):** Bornyl isovalerate and other esters; contribute characteristic odor and mild sedative/nervine action.[web:21]  
- **Iridoids/Valepotriates (unstable):** Historically implicated in anxiolytic/sedative action; break down with high heat or long storage.[web:13]  
- **Flavonoids and lignans:** Antioxidant support, minor contribution to neuroprotection.[web:21]  

**Mechanisms:**  
- **GABAergic modulation:** Valerenic acids increase GABA availability and bind to GABA-A receptor subunits, enhancing inhibitory neurotransmission and reducing neuronal excitability.[web:21]  
- **Anxiolytic/antidepressant-like:** Mild inhibition of GABA reuptake and interaction with serotonin receptors; improves stress resilience and subjective anxiety.[web:16][web:21]  
- **Spasmolytic:** Smooth muscle relaxation in GI and uterine tissues; may ease tension headaches and crampy pain patterns.[web:6]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. Sedative/Hypnotic (Valerenic Acids — PRIMARY)**  
Valerenic acids and related constituents enhance GABAergic tone in the CNS, shortening sleep-onset latency and improving sleep maintenance without strong “drugged” feeling when dosed appropriately.[web:21]  
Effect: Easier sleep onset, deeper rest, reduced nocturnal awakenings in anxiety-related insomnia.  
Timeline: Often some benefit within days; full effect commonly after 2–4 weeks of consistent use.[web:21]  
Clinical significance: Excellent when insomnia is linked to mental overactivity, stress, and tension rather than severe primary sleep disorders.

**2. Anxiolytic/Calming (Daytime Nervine)**  
At moderate doses, valerian can reduce subjective anxiety, muscle tension, and restlessness without causing strong daytime sedation in many individuals.[web:21]  
Effect: Calmer baseline, less reactivity, gently smoothed stress response.  
Timeline: Cumulative over 1–3 weeks; may also have acute calming effect for situational anxiety.

**3. Antispasmodic (Smooth Muscle Relaxant)**  
Valerian preparations exhibit spasmolytic effects on smooth muscle (GI, uterine), likely via calcium channel modulation and GABAergic effects.[web:6]  
Effect: Reduction in tension headaches, menstrual cramps, and stress-related gut spasms.

**4. Nervine Tonic (Longer-Term)**  
Ongoing use in appropriate doses supports a more balanced stress response and nervous system resilience, particularly when combined with lifestyle and sleep hygiene practices.[web:21]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Dosage Range:**  

**Tea/Infusion:**  
- 2–3 g dried root per 250 ml water, steeped 10–15 minutes, 1–2× daily, usually evening.  
- Less efficient for some lipophilic constituents but useful when alcohol is contraindicated.[web:21]

**Extract (1:5 in 40–70% alcohol):**  
- 2–4 ml (approx. 40–80 drops), 1–3× daily.  
- For insomnia: full dose 30–60 minutes before bed; optional smaller dose in late afternoon to “soften the landing.”[web:21]

**Standardized Extract (Capsules/Tablets):**  
- 300–600 mg extract (e.g., standardized to 0.8% valerenic acids) taken 30–60 minutes before bedtime.[web:21]  

**Duration:**  
- Common clinical use: 2–4 weeks continuous to evaluate full effect.  
- Many monographs recommend limiting continuous high-dose use to 4–6 weeks before tapering or cycling, particularly in sensitive individuals.[web:6][web:21]

**Best Preparations & Protocols:**  

**Protocol 1: Anxiety-Related Insomnia (Short–Moderate Term)**  
- 300–600 mg standardized extract or 3–4 ml extract 30–60 minutes before bed.  
- Optionally 1–2 ml extract in late afternoon if evening anxiety ramps up.  
- Duration: 2–6 weeks, then reassess and taper if appropriate.[web:21]

**Protocol 2: Daytime Anxiety & Tension**  
- 1–2 ml extract up to 3× daily for mild-moderate anxiety with muscle tension.  
- Combine with Milky oats or Lemon balm for “soft-focus” calm rather than heavy sedation.

**Protocol 3: Tension Headache/Muscle Spasm**  
- 2–3 ml extract at onset, repeat every few hours as needed (within daily limits).  
- Pair with magnesium, skullcap, or cramp bark depending on pattern.

**Protocol 4: Perimenopausal Sleep Disturbance**  
- 300–600 mg extract at bedtime, optionally combined with hops or passionflower.  
- Support thermoregulation and stress patterns in parallel.

## KEY BENEFITS

- **Sleep Support:** Improves sleep quality and reduces sleep latency in mild to moderate insomnia, particularly when stress-related.[web:6][web:21]  
- **Anxiolytic:** Eases nervous tension, restlessness, and stress-related agitation.[web:16][web:21]  
- **Nervine Tonic:** Gradually supports more resilient stress response with appropriate use.  
- **Antispasmodic:** Helpful for tension headaches, menstrual cramps, and some functional GI spasm patterns.[web:6]  
- **Non-Habit-Forming (When Used Properly):** Does not act as a classical benzodiazepine; dependence risk is much lower when doses and duration are reasonable.[web:21]

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:**  
GRADE B — Generally safe for short-term use in adults; caution with long-term high-dose use and with sedative drugs.[web:6][web:21]

**Contraindications (or Strong Cautions):**  
- Pregnancy and lactation: Insufficient safety data — avoid or use only under professional supervision.[web:21]  
- Children under 12: Often not recommended in standardized doses; if used, very low doses and professional guidance.  
- Known hypersensitivity to Valeriana species.[web:16]

**Cautions:**  
- Pre-existing liver disease: Some reports of hepatotoxicity in multi-herb preparations; monitor or use alternatives in significant hepatic compromise.[web:16]  
- Taper after long continuous high-dose use to avoid rebound sleeplessness or agitation in sensitive individuals.[web:21]  

**Herb-to-Herb Synergy (Beneficial):**  
- **Passionflower, Hops, Lemon balm, Skullcap:** Synergistic for sleep and anxiety formulas.  
- **Milky Oats:** Deep nervous system nourishment paired with acute calming.

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**  
- May potentiate CNS depressants: benzodiazepines, barbiturates, sedative antihistamines, alcohol, some sleep medications.[web:16][web:21]  
- Possible CYP3A4 modulation suggested; exercise caution with narrow-therapeutic-index drugs (though data are limited).[web:16]

## CLINICAL NOTES & PRACTITIONER GUIDANCE

- **Quality & Sourcing:** Prefer preparations specifying valerenic acid content; avoid very old stock (odor fades). Extracts and standardized extracts generally more reliable than simple tea for consistent effect.[web:13][web:21]  
- **Onset Expectations:** Set expectation that full benefit may take 2–4 weeks of consistent nighttime use; avoid “failed in one night” narratives.  
- **Patient Matching:** Best in wired-tired, anxious, overthinking constitutions rather than deeply depleted, cold, or very hypotensive patients.  
- **Combination Strategy:**  
  - With passionflower for racing thoughts.  
  - With hops for heavier sedation in short-term severe insomnia (not chronic).  
  - With milky oats for long-haul burnout.  
- **Driving/Machinery:** Advise caution until individual response known; some experience morning grogginess on higher doses.[web:21]

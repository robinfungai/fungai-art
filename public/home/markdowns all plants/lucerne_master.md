# LUCERNE / ALFALFA  
*Medicago sativa*

---

## OVERVIEW

**Common Names:** Lucerne, Alfalfa  
**Plant Part:** Leaf and aerial parts (fresh or dried)  
**Primary Actions:** Nutritive tonic; mineral and protein rich; gentle hormonal and cardiometabolic support.[web:139][web:145][web:150]

Lucerne is a deep‑rooted legume that concentrates minerals, vitamins, and phytonutrients from the soil, making it a classic “green food” tonic in Western herbalism and modern nutrition.[web:139][web:140][web:145]

---

## KEY CONSTITUENTS

- Proteins and essential amino acids  
- Vitamins: K (high), C, some B‑vitamins, provitamin A carotenoids  
- Minerals: calcium, magnesium, iron, potassium, trace minerals  
- Saponins, coumarins, phytoestrogens (isoflavones + coumestans)  
- Chlorophyll and other green pigments[web:139][web:140][web:150]

These constituents explain its use as a **nutritive, remineralizing tonic** and its mild effects on lipids, blood sugar, and hormones.[web:139][web:141][web:150]

---

## TRADITIONAL & MODERN USES

### 1. Nutritive Tonification & Minerals

- Used as a **tonic food** for convalescence, general weakness, poor diet, and long‑term mineral depletion.  
- Supports hair, nails, teeth, and connective tissue via mineral content.[web:139][web:141][web:145]  
- Often combined with Nettle, Oatstraw, Yellow Dock, and Moringa as a **foundational nutrient base**.

### 2. Cardiometabolic Support

- Saponins and fiber can support **cholesterol and triglyceride modulation**, with small studies suggesting LDL reduction and improved lipid profiles.[web:141][web:143][web:150]  
- Mild blood‑sugar support and antioxidant activity contribute to long‑term cardiovascular health.[web:139][web:145]

### 3. Digestive & “Detox” Support

- Traditionally used for **digestive weakness, atony, and mild constipation**, acting as a gentle, fiber‑rich tonic.[web:141][web:148]  
- Supports liver and gallbladder indirectly via improved bile flow and gut motility in some traditional sources.

### 4. Women’s Health & Endocrine Modulation

- Contains phytoestrogens that may support **menopausal transitions, bone density, and menstrual regularity**, though evidence is modest.[web:141][web:172]  
- Sometimes used in blends for **PMS, perimenopause, and post‑menopausal support**, paired with Shatavari, Red Clover, and Vitex.

---

## ENERGETICS & TISSUE STATE

- **Temperature:** Neutral to slightly cooling  
- **Moisture:** Slightly moistening  
- **Taste:** Mild, green, slightly sweet‑grassy  
- **Tissue States:** Atrophy and deficiency; dryness; nutritional depletion; mild stagnation from low vitality.

Best suited for **deficient, under‑nourished, overworked, or convalescent** constitutions needing long‑term rebuilding rather than acute stimulation.

---

## PREPARATIONS & DOSING

### Infusion (Tea)

- 2–4 g dried leaf per 250 ml boiling water  
- Steep 10–20 minutes; drink 1–3 cups daily as a tonic.[web:139][web:145]

### Extract

- Ratio: 1:3–1:5 in 25–40% alcohol (fresh or well‑dried herb)  
- Dose: 2–5 ml, 1–3x daily as part of nutritive or women’s‑health blends.

### Powder / Food

- 1–5 g/day powdered herb, added to smoothies or foods, often combined with other green powders.[web:139][web:145]

Long‑term, low‑dose daily use is typical, more like **food** than a short‑course “medicine.”

---

## SAFETY, CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**General:**  
- Considered very safe as food/herb in healthy adults; widely used in supplements and animal feed.[web:139][web:145]

**Cautions:**  
- **Autoimmune conditions / SLE (lupus):** Rare reports of **L‑canavanine** in alfalfa sprouts exacerbating lupus‑like symptoms; avoid high long‑term doses in known SLE or autoimmune flares.[web:141][web:143][web:199]  
- **Anticoagulant therapy (Warfarin, etc.):** High vitamin K content may **interfere with INR control**; use cautiously and coordinate with prescribing provider.[web:139][web:145]  
- **Hormone‑sensitive cancers:** Phytoestrogen content suggests caution in estrogen‑receptor‑positive cancers; individualize based on oncology guidance.[web:141][web:172]

**Pregnancy & Lactation:**  
- Traditionally used as a nutrient tonic; modern sources generally regard moderate food‑like amounts as safe, but high‑dose supplements should be practitioner‑guided.[web:139][web:141]

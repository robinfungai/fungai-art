# SLIPPERY ELM (Ulmus rubra) – INNER BARK

## Master Monograph: Demulcent for Respiratory & GI Tracts

**Primary Species:** Ulmus rubra Muhl. (slippery elm, red elm).[web:268][web:274]  
**Family:** Ulmaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Inner bark**, dried and powdered or cut.[web:268][web:274]  

### Functional Overview

- Energetics: Cooling, moistening.  
- Actions: **Demulcent, emollient, mild anti‑inflammatory, nutritive.**[web:268][web:274]  
- Main uses: Soothing **esophagitis/GERD, gastritis, IBD flares, diarrhea, cough, sore throat, urinary irritation**.[web:268][web:274][web:261]

### Pharmacology & Effects

- Rich in **mucilaginous polysaccharides**, which coat and protect irritated mucosa (GI, respiratory, urinary).[web:268][web:274]  
- Case and clinical experience suggest symptom relief in IBD, heartburn, sore throat, and cough; evidence base is mostly traditional and small observational work.[web:261][web:263][web:274]  

### Dosing

- Cold‑or warm‑water slurry: 1–2 tsp powder in water, 1–3× daily between meals for GERD/IBD/UTIs.[web:261][web:263][web:264]  
- Lozenges / teas for throat and cough as per product labels.

### Safety & Pregnancy

- Generally **well tolerated**; FDA “GRAS” status for oral use.[web:261]  
- Main issue: can **reduce absorption of medications/supplements**; separate by ≥2 hours.[web:263][web:264][web:271]  
- Pregnancy: data conflict. Some modern sources say oral use appears safe, but folklore of bark strips inserted into cervix causing miscarriage led to a reputation for abortifacient risk; most conservative references advise **avoiding during pregnancy and breastfeeding**.[web:261][web:262][web:265][web:271]  
- Not recommended for infants or small children due to limited data.[web:261]

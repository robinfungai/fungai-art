# WORMWOOD (Artemisia absinthium)
## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Artemisia absinthium L. (Grand Wormwood, Absinthe Wormwood) — European native; temperate regions worldwide. Perennial semi-woody shrub; **above-ground plant** (aerial parts: leaves + flowering tops, NOT roots) harvested at flowering (July-August); dried at low temperature; used in absinthe liqueur (infamous historical context).
**Botanical Family:** Asteraceae (daisy/aster family)
**Part Used:** Artemisia absinthium herba — **Above-ground plant only** (leaves + flowering tops), **NOT root**. Harvested at peak flowering (maximum thujone + absinthin content).
**Harvesting & Processing:** Collected at flowering peak; dried immediately at low temperature to preserve volatile compounds; stored airtight away from light (volatile alkaloids sensitive to heat/light).
**Quality Indicators:** Silvery-green leaves; yellow flowers; distinctive pungent aroma (thujone signature). Potency indicated by strong scent + color.
**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark location; volatile alkaloids volatile—maintain potency 12-18 months (shorter than non-volatile herbs).
**Historical Context:** Used in European herbalism centuries (medieval); infamous in absinthe liqueur (thujone toxicity concerns from alcohol-enhanced CNS penetration, NOT herb alone); modern herbalism re-evaluating with appropriate caution.

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**
**Primary Bioactives:**
- **Thujone (α & β—Volatile Alkaloid, Neurotoxic—0.3-1.5% dried herb, 50%+ in essential oil—PRIMARY CONCERN):** Antiparasitic, antimicrobial, convulsant at high doses. **Neurotoxic potential at excessive doses** (crosses BBB at high concentrations).
- **Absinthin (Sesquiterpene Lactone—Bitter Compound, 0.2-0.8%—ANTIPARASITIC PRIMARY):** Antiparasitic (rounds + tapeworms particularly), antimicrobial, bitter digestive stimulant.
**Supporting Compounds:**
- **Azulene (Anti-inflammatory, 0.1-0.3%):** Reduces TNF-α, IL-6; anti-inflammatory.
- **Flavonoids (Antioxidant—Minor):** Free radical scavenging.

**Mechanisms:**
- **Antiparasitic (Thujone + Absinthin—PRIMARY MECHANISM):** Thujone + absinthin immobilize parasites (particularly roundworms + tapeworms). Sesquiterpene lactones disrupt parasite metabolic processes. Mechanism: cell membrane damage, enzyme inhibition.
- **Antimicrobial (Thujone—Broad Spectrum):** Bactericidal + fungicidal activity (E. coli, Staphylococcus, Candida, etc.).
- **Digestive Bitters (Absinthin—Intense):** Bitter taste receptors → cascade HCl, pepsin, bile, pancreatic enzyme release. Powerful digestive stimulation; appetite stimulation (pre-meal use).
- **Liver Choleretic (Sesquiterpene Lactones):** Stimulates bile production + flow; moves stagnant bile. Liver function enhancement.
- **Anti-inflammatory (Azulene):** Reduces inflammatory cytokines; anti-inflammatory support.
- **Neurotoxicity Concern (Thujone—CRITICAL CAUTION):** Thujone crosses blood-brain barrier (BBB) at high doses (concentrated essential oil particularly). Neurotoxic effects: convulsions at extreme doses. **ABSINTHE toxicity = alcohol + thujone synergy** (alcohol enhanced CNS penetration). Herbal therapeutic doses (0.3-1.5% in whole herb, limiting dose + duration) generally safe short-term; long-term high-dose = seizure risk.

## ⚠️ CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS — CRITICAL

**Safety Profile:** GRADE C (HIGH CAUTION NEEDED) — Effective + safe SHORT-TERM with appropriate restrictions; serious risks with excessive use, duration overrun, or specific populations.

**ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATIONS:**
**PREGNANCY — ABSOLUTELY AVOID (Abortifacient + Neurotoxic to Fetus):** Thujone is uterine stimulant (emmenagogue) + potentially neurotoxic to developing fetus. **Miscarriage risk + fetal CNS damage risk.** **ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED all trimesters.**
**BREASTFEEDING — AVOID:** Thujone present in breast milk; infant exposure = convulsion risk.
**SEIZURE DISORDERS — ABSOLUTELY AVOID (Convulsant Risk):** Thujone is documented convulsant at high doses; crosses BBB. **Seizure risk significant.** **ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED.**

**MAJOR CAUTIONS:**
**Kidney Disease:** Thujone nephrotoxic (kidney damage potential); contraindication if kidney function compromised.
**Liver Disease:** Intense bitter + hepatotoxic potential (sesquiterpene lactone). Monitor if liver dysfunction.
**On Anticonvulsants:** Thujone may reduce anticonvulsant efficacy; seizure breakthrough risk. Caution/avoid.
**Bleeding Disorders:** Anticoagulant activity (theoretical); caution.

**Herb-to-Herb Interactions:**
**Antiparasitic Trio (Synergistic—Recommended):** Black Walnut Hulls + Clove + Wormwood = potent antiparasitic combination. Synergistic; reduces single-herb doses.
**⚠️ CRITICAL — Other Thujone-Rich Herbs (Mugwort, Sage High-Dose):** Cumulative thujone toxicity risk with multiple high-thujone herbs simultaneously. **Avoid combining** or use extreme caution + reduce doses.

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**
**Anticonvulsants:** Thujone may reduce drug efficacy; seizure breakthrough risk.
**Anticoagulants:** Theoretical additive; caution.

## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:** Uncaria tomentosa (Willd. ex Roem. & Schult.) DC. (Cat's Claw, Uña de Gato) — Peruvian Amazon native; woody climbing vine with distinctive claw-shaped thorns resembling cat claws. **Bark + Root Bark** primary medicinal use; fresh or dried; traditional anti-inflammatory + immune herb (centuries indigenous, modern Western discovery 1990s).

**Botanical Family:** Rubiaceae (coffee/madder family)

**Part Used:** Uncaria tomentosa cortex (bark) + root bark—**Inner bark preferred** (highest alkaloid concentration); whole dried bark preferred (higher bioactivity). Bark/root bark = most medicinal parts.

**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvested year-round from mature vines (sustainability concern—wild harvesting depletes populations); dried at low temperature to preserve alkaloids; whole bark preferred. Fresh bark tincture most potent.

**Quality Indicators:** Brown/rust color (dried); distinctive herbal aroma; bitter taste (alkaloid marker). Potency indicated by bark color intensity + bitterness (inner bark = darker/more bitter).

**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark; maintains potency 12-18 months. Proper storage important (alkaloids stable but can degrade).

**Historical Context:** Peruvian Amazon indigenous use centuries (anti-inflammatory, immune); modern Western discovery 1990s; contemporary research (200+ studies on alkaloids, immune, anti-inflammatory, DNA repair, cancer prevention—preliminary); major modern herbalism focus; popular supplement in Western market.

## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:** Capsicum annuum L. (Cayenne, Red Chili Pepper, Capsicum) — Central American native; annual herbaceous plant producing pungent fruits. **Dried fruits (pods)** primary medicinal use; fresh or dried; pungent heat intensity variable (Scoville rating indicates potency).

**Botanical Family:** Solanaceae (nightshade family)

**Part Used:** Capsicum annuum fructum — **Dried fruits (pods)** only; whole dried pods or powdered; fresh fruits highest potency (fresh alkaloid concentration). Potency measured in Scoville Heat Units (SHU) or percentage capsaicinoids.

**Harvesting & Processing:** Fruits harvested autumn at full ripeness (peak capsaicinoid concentration); dried at low temperature (heat-drying preserves capsaicinoids); whole pods stored; ground into powder. Quality indicated by deep red color + pungent aroma (strong smell = high capsaicinoid content).

**Quality Indicators:** Deep red color; distinctively pungent, hot aroma; extremely hot taste (Scoville scale variable: 30,000-190,000+ SHU depending on variety). Potency directly indicated by heat intensity (hotness = capsaicinoid concentration).

**Storage:** Airtight container, cool dark, protected from light + heat (light + moisture degrade capsaicinoids); maintains potency 18-24 months (volatile compound—degradation possible).

**Historical Context:** Mesoamerican native use (5000+ years); post-Columbian global spread; traditional medicine (warming, circulation); culinary staple (worldwide); modern research (300+ studies on pain, metabolism, cardiovascular, anti-inflammatory).

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Dosage Range (POTENT—Start LOW—CAUTION):**

**Extract (Fresh Plant—MOST CONTROLLED):** 2-5 drops MAXIMUM in water with food. **EXTREMELY POTENT—start 2 drops; assess tolerance before increasing.** More than 5 drops = risk of excessive stimulation + BP elevation. Most controlled + safest dosing form.

**Powder (Dried—LESS CONTROLLED):** 1/8 to 1/4 teaspoon (pinch to small amount) ONLY. **NEVER straight powder (will burn mouth + throat).** Mix into food/soup. Harder to control dosing than extract.

**Fresh Powder in Food (Culinary):** Cook into soup/stew (heat preserves capsaicinoids; integrates with food). More palateable; mild effect from culinary quantities.

**ALWAYS with Food (CRITICAL—Prevents Irritation):** NEVER on empty stomach (severe stomach irritation + pain possible). ALWAYS consume with substantial food.

**Timing:** Morning preferred (stimulating); NOT evening (sleep disruption risk; stimulating properties). Single dose daily or every other day maximum.

## ⚠️ CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS — CRITICAL WARNINGS

**Safety Profile:** GRADE C (Potent; multiple contraindications; high-caution herb) — Requires careful screening + monitoring.

**ABSOLUTE CONTRAINDICATIONS (DO NOT USE—RED FLAG):**

**BLEEDING DISORDERS — ABSOLUTELY AVOID:** Capsaicin increases bleeding risk (antiplatelet activity). **CONTRAINDICATED.** Risk: Excessive bleeding, hemorrhage.

**ON WARFARIN (Blood Thinner—ABSOLUTELY AVOID):** Capsaicin antiplatelet + Warfarin anticoagulant = additive bleeding risk (DANGEROUS combination). **CONTRAINDICATED without medical supervision.** **MUST consult hematologist/anticoagulation specialist before considering use.**

**PEPTIC ULCERS / GERD (ACID REFLUX)—ABSOLUTELY AVOID:** Cayenne irritating; can worsen ulcers + GERD symptoms; causes severe pain in affected individuals. **CONTRAINDICATED.**

**MAJOR CAUTIONS:**

**HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE (HYPERTENSION—CAUTION + MONITORING CRITICAL):** Cayenne can spike BP (stimulating, thermogenic). If hypertensive or on BP medications: **MANDATORY BP monitoring; consult provider; may need to avoid or use extremely low dose (2 drops) with intensive monitoring.**

**Sensitive Stomach / GI Inflammation (CAUTION):** Cayenne irritating; can cause gastric pain, acid reflux exacerbation. Use cautiously if GI-sensitive; take with substantial food; monitor symptoms.

**Heart Arrhythmia / Cardiac Conditions (CAUTION):** Stimulating properties; sympathomimetic effect; may trigger arrhythmias in susceptible individuals. **Consult cardiologist; monitor.**

**Anxiety Disorders (CAUTION):** Stimulating; may exacerbate anxiety in sensitive individuals. **Monitor response; start low; avoid if already anxious.**

**Insomnia (AVOID EVENING):** Stimulating; avoid evening use (sleep disruption likely). Morning-only use.

**Pregnancy (CAUTION—Traditional Abortifacient):** Traditional use as uterine stimulant (high doses); modern data limited. **Consult prenatal provider; avoid high doses; culinary amounts likely safe.**

**Breastfeeding (CAUTION):** Irritating to infant if passed through breast milk; limit use. **Consult provider.**

**Herb-to-Herb Interactions (CRITICAL):**

**⚠️ HORNY GOAT WEED + DAMIANA (DANGEROUS—OVERSTIMULATION):** Together = excessive stimulation + heat + BP elevation. **AVOID combination.** Use separately or use extremely low doses.

**⚠️ Other Stimulants (Ginseng Root, High-Dose Caffeine—CAUTION):** Cumulative stimulation; excessive sympathetic activation; BP elevation. Moderate combination or avoid.

**Monitor with:**
- **Ginger (Warming—Additive):** Both warming; okay in small amounts together but not high doses.
- **Garlic (Antiplatelet—CAUTION):** Additive antiplatelet effect; monitor if also on anticoagulants.

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions (CRITICAL—MANY):**

**⚠️ WARFARIN (Blood Thinner—CONTRAINDICATION):** Additive anticoagulation; bleeding risk (DANGEROUS). **MUST consult hematologist; likely contraindicated.**

**⚠️ Antiplatelet Drugs (Aspirin, Clopidogrel—ADDITIVE):** Cumulative antiplatelet effect; bleeding risk. **Monitor; consult prescriber.**

**Blood Pressure Medications (Additive BP Lowering—PARADOXICAL):** While Cayenne can spike BP acutely, chronic use may lower BP (vasodilation). Combined with BP-lowering meds = excessive BP drop risk. **Monitor BP regularly; may need medication adjustment.**

**Diabetes Medications (MONITOR—Possible Blood Sugar Effects):** Theoretical blood sugar modulation; monitor glucose levels.

**Proton Pump Inhibitors (Acid Reflux Meds—CONTRAINDICATION):** Taking Cayenne while managing acid reflux = counterproductive (Cayenne irritating). **Avoid if on PPI.**

**NSAIDs (Pain Meds—MONITOR GI IRRITATION):** Both irritating to GI; combined use increases ulcer risk. **Monitor GI symptoms; consult prescriber.**

**Stimulant Medications (ADHD meds, Decongestants—ADDITIVE):** Cumulative stimulation; excessive sympathetic activation. **Monitor; may cause overstimulation.**

## CLINICAL NOTES & PRACTITIONER GUIDANCE

**Screening Non-Negotiable:** This is THE herb requiring most thorough screening. Bleeding disorders, Warfarin, ulcers = absolute blocks. Hypertension = CRITICAL monitoring requirement.

**Low Dosing Essential:** 2-3 drops extract is MAXIMUM starting dose for most users. Many practitioners overshoot dosing (5+ drops) → excessive stimulation + BP elevation + GI irritation. Extract form ESSENTIAL (powders too hard to dose accurately).

**BP Monitoring Mandatory:** If recommending to anyone, require regular BP monitoring (home BP cuff). Document BP baseline + ongoing. Medical-grade caution.

**Cycling Critical:** Daily use develops tolerance (TRPV1 receptor downregulation). Cycle use (5 on, 2 off) prevents tolerance + maintains efficacy. Educate clients on cycling importance.

**Hematology Consultation:** If client on Warfarin or has bleeding disorder, referral to hematologist BEFORE recommending Cayenne (not herbal practitioner decision—medical decision).

**Not Casual Herb:** Cayenne is NOT beginner herb. Requires education, screening, monitoring, intention. "Fire" element herb = demands respect + caution.

**Acute vs. Chronic:** Frame as acute circulation catalyst + pain relief (5-7 days acute use; cycling chronic use). NOT daily long-term foundational herb.

## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:** Crocus sativus L. (Saffron, Crocus, Red Gold) — Mediterranean native (Iran largest producer); perennial flowering plant. **Stigmas only** (the three red threads per flower) primary medicinal use; dried at low temperature; historically precious spice + medicine.

**Botanical Family:** Iridaceae (iris family)

**Part Used:** Crocus sativus stigma — **Stigmas only** (the three red filaments from each flower; NO other plant parts used). Hand-harvested, dried at low temperature; extremely labor-intensive (requires ~150 flowers for 1g saffron—explains premium cost). Only part with significant bioactives.

**Harvesting & Processing:** Flowers harvested autumn at dawn (peak alkaloid concentration); stigmas hand-separated (100% manual labor); dried at low temperature (<40°C—critical to preserve crocin); whole stigmas stored. 1-2 grams saffron = 300-400 flowers (explains cost: $8-15 per gram retail).

**Quality Indicators:** Deep red color (crocin pigment—color = potency); characteristic warm, slightly sweet aroma; distinctive bitter-sweet taste. Potency indicated by color intensity (deeper red = higher crocin concentration). Laboratory testing for crocin % recommended (ISO 3632 grading system).

**Storage:** Airtight, light-proof glass, cool dark, protected from light + humidity (light + moisture degrade crocin); maintains potency 18-24 months (stable if properly stored). Proper storage critical due to cost.

**Historical Context:** Ancient spice (3000+ years—Persia, India, Mediterranean); "red gold" (premium cost); medieval European medicine (mood + vitality); modern research (200+ studies on mood, antioxidant, neuroprotection). Traditionally precious medicine + luxury spice.

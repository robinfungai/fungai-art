## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:** Acorus calamus L. (Calamus, Sweet Flag, Myrtle Flag, Rat Root) — Asian/North American native (two subspecies: Asian variety higher β-asarone; North American variety lower β-asarone); aquatic/wetland plant with distinctive tall, sword-like green leaves. **Rhizome** primary medicinal use; fresh or dried; traditional cognitive + consciousness + spiritual herb (centuries traditional, MODERN REGULATORY CONCERNS). **⚠️ NEUROTOXICITY CONCERN—DOSE-DEPENDENT—RESTRICTED/BANNED IN SOME JURISDICTIONS.**

**Botanical Family:** Araceae (arum family)

**Part Used:** Acorus calamus rhizoma (rhizome—underground storage stem)—**Dried rhizome optimal** (concentrates bioactive compounds); fresh rhizome also potent; whole dried rhizome preferred (higher bioactivity). Rhizome = only medicinal part.

**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvested in autumn (optimal rhizome maturity); dried at low temperature to preserve volatile compounds + alkaloids; whole rhizome preferred. Dried rhizome stable long-term.

**Quality Indicators:** Light tan/brown color (dried); distinctive aromatic, sweet, slightly medicinal aroma (essential oil); warm, bitter taste (alkaloid + aromatic marker). Potency indicated by aroma intensity (stronger aroma = higher essential oil + volatile concentration).

**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark, protected from light (volatile compounds degrade); maintains bioactivity 12-18 months. Proper storage important.

**Historical Context:** Traditional use centuries in Ayurvedic medicine (cognitive, consciousness, meditation), Chinese medicine (spirit opening, cognitive clarity), North American herbalism (consciousness, memory); modern Western rediscovery 1960s-1970s (psychonautic communities); contemporary research (40+ studies on β-asarone neurotoxicity, cognitive mechanisms, consciousness effects); HIGHLY CONTROVERSIAL (traditional benefits vs. modern safety concerns from β-asarone content).

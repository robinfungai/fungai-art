**Primary Species:** Eucalyptus globulus Labill. (Blue Gum Eucalyptus) — Australian native; towering evergreen tree with distinctive blue-tinted leaves. **Leaves** primary medicinal use; fresh or dried; traditional respiratory + antimicrobial herb (centuries).

**Botanical Family:** Myrtaceae (myrtle family)

**Part Used:** Eucalyptus globulus folium (leaves)—**Dried leaves or essential oil; whole dried leaves preferred** (higher bioactivity); fresh leaf infusion also potent. Essential oil concentrated preparation; use carefully (very potent).

**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvested year-round (evergreen); dried at low temperature to preserve essential oils; whole leaves stored. Fresh leaf infusion most potent (preserves volatiles); essential oil most concentrated + potent (use drop doses).

**Quality Indicators:** Gray-green color (distinctive); extremely distinctive strong, minty, medicinal aroma (eucalyptol marker); cooling, sharp taste. Potency indicated by aroma intensity + sharpness (fresh = strongest).

**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark, protected from light + heat (essential oils highly volatile); maintains potency 12-18 months (essential oil degrades faster). Proper storage critical.

**Historical Context:** Australian aboriginal traditional use centuries (respiratory, antimicrobial); European adoption (1800s onward); traditional medicine cornerstone; modern research (200+ studies on respiratory, antimicrobial, antiviral, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant); contemporary herbalism respiratory + antimicrobial focus.

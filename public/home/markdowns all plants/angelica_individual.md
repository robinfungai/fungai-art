# ANGELICA / DONG QUAI (Angelica sinensis)
## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Angelica sinensis (Oliv.) Diels (Dong Quai, Chinese Angelica, Female Ginseng) — East Asian native (China, Korea, Japan mountainous regions). Biennial plant; **root** harvested 2-3 years after planting (maturity required); dried at low temperature; staple of Traditional Chinese Medicine.
**Botanical Family:** Apiaceae (parsley/umbelliferous family)
**Part Used:** Angelica sinensis radix — **Root only** (concentrated bioactives; 2-3 year matured optimal).
**Harvesting & Processing:** Roots dug at plant maturity (2-3 years); dried immediately at low temperature to preserve delicate compounds; stored airtight away from light.
**Quality Indicators:** Dark brown to blackish color; distinctive sweet aromatic (ligustilide signature); dense texture. Potency indicated by aroma + color.
**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark location; maintains potency 18-24 months.
**Historical Context:** Used in Traditional Chinese Medicine 2000+ years; one of most valued women's health herbs; called "female ginseng" (Chinese: dang gui).

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:** Menstrual regulation (heavy, light, irregular, painful), blood tonification (energy, vitality), circulation enhancement, dysmenorrhea relief, cardiovascular support, neuroprotection (secondary).

**⚠️ CRITICAL DRUG INTERACTION:**
**WARFARIN — ABSOLUTE AVOID (INCREASES INR SIGNIFICANTLY; DOCUMENTED SERIOUS BLEEDING):** Angelica's antiplatelet activity (ferulic acid) + anticoagulation property increases warfarin INR significantly. **Documented serious bleeding cases.** Many sources recommend ABSOLUTE AVOIDANCE of combination. **If combination necessary, INR monitoring MANDATORY + frequent (weekly) + warfarin dose likely reduced.** **Consult cardiologist/anticoagulation specialist.**

**MAJOR CAUTIONS:**
**Other Anticoagulants (Aspirin, Clopidogrel, Dabigatran, Apixaban—MONITOR):** Additive antiplatelet/anticoagulation effects. Monitor bleeding risk.
**First Trimester Pregnancy — AVOID:** Uterine stimulation risk (emmenagogue activity); miscarriage risk. Later pregnancy safer (traditional use).
**Breastfeeding:** Insufficient modern data; traditional use suggests OK. If uncertain, consult provider.

**Herb-to-Herb Interactions:**
**Vitex (Chasteberry—Synergistic):** Both regulate menstrual cycle; complementary mechanisms.
**Cramp Bark (Pain Relief—Synergistic):** Both relieve period pain; synergistic antispasmodic + anti-inflammatory action.
**Ginger (Warming—Synergistic):** Both warming + circulation-enhancing; excellent pairing.
**Ginkgo (Circulation—Synergistic):** Both improve circulation; synergistic for poor circulation + Raynaud's.
**⚠️ Other Anticoagulant Herbs (High Caution):** Ginkgo, Garlic, Turmeric (all have antiplatelet activity)—avoid stacking with Angelica if also on anticoagulant medications.

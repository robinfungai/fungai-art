## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Glechoma hederacea L. (Ground ivy, Gill‑over‑the‑ground, Alehoof) — Creeping perennial mint native to Europe and Western Asia, naturalized widely; aromatic, scalloped leaves and small purple flowers.[web:130][web:133][web:136]  
**Botanical Family:** Lamiaceae (mint family).[web:130]  
**Part Used:** **Aerial parts** (leaves, stems, flowering tops) fresh or dried.[web:130][web:133]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvest in spring or early flowering; dry quickly in shade with airflow. Often extractd fresh for stronger aromatics.[web:130][web:133]  
**Quality Indicators:** Bright to deep green leaves, aromatic minty/resinous scent with a slightly bitter note; no mold or excessive browning. Fresh plant extracts retain strong aroma.[web:130][web:133]  
**Storage:** Dried herb in airtight containers, cool/dark; best within 1 year. Extract stable several years in dark glass.[web:130]  
**Historical Context:** Traditional European remedy for **catarrh (ear, nose, throat congestion), chronic bronchitis, digestive upset, and kidney/bladder issues**; also historically used in brewing (“alehoof”) to clarify and flavor beer before hops.[web:130][web:133]

**Mechanisms:**

- **Antioxidant & Cytotoxic:**

**1. Anticatarrhal & Expectorant (Sinus, Ear, Lung)**  
Aromatic and astringent constituents loosen and dry excess mucus, improving drainage from ENT and bronchial passages.[web:130][web:133]  
Effect: Reduced congestion, postnasal drip, “glue ear,” and chronic catarrh, especially in cold/damp patterns.  
Timeline: Days to weeks of regular use.

**2. Mild Bitter Digestive & Hepatic Support**  
Ground ivy stimulates digestion slightly and helps clear “thick, sticky” GI mucus; often combined with other bitters.[web:130][web:133]  
Effect: Improved appetite, reduced bloating, improved assimilation in damp, sluggish digestion.  
Timeline: Weeks.

**3. Antioxidant & Antimicrobial**  
In vitro studies show strong antioxidant activity and notable antimicrobial effects, with some dose‑dependent cytotoxicity at high concentrations.[web:136]  
Effect: Supports host defenses and mucosal resilience in infections; theoretical anti‑cancer and cytotoxic roles, but not well studied clinically.

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:**  
- **Anticatarrhal** for sinus/ear/lung congestion.[web:130][web:133]  
- Mild digestive and urinary support in damp, mucousy patterns.  
- Antioxidant and antimicrobial potential.[web:136]

**Safety Overview:**  

- For humans, above‑ground parts generally considered **safe in moderate doses**, though caution is warranted due to cytotoxic effects at high extract concentrations in vitro.[web:130][web:136]  
- Toxic to some **livestock (especially horses)** when grazed in large amounts.[web:133]

**⚠️ CRITICAL CAUTIONS**

- **Pregnancy & Lactation:**  
  - Limited data; generally recommended to avoid high‑dose medicinal use in pregnancy; food‑like amounts probably safe.[web:130]

- **Children:**  
  - Moderate doses of tea or low‑dose extract may be used by experienced practitioners; avoid high doses due to limited data and cytotoxic signals in vitro.[web:130][web:136]

**MAJOR CAUTIONS**

- **Liver Disease / Hepatotoxic Risk:**  
  - In vitro cytotoxicity in HepG2 liver cells at higher concentrations suggests caution in liver disease and with prolonged high dosing.[web:136]

- **Allergy:**  
  - Lamiaceae allergies possible; discontinue if rash or respiratory symptoms arise.

**Drug Interactions:**  
- No well‑documented clinical interactions; theoretical caution with hepatotoxic drugs or chemo due to cytotoxicity and hepatic metabolism considerations.

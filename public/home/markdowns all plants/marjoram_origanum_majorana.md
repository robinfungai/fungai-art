# MARJORAM (Origanum majorana) – SWEET MARJORAM

## Master Monograph

**Primary Species:** Origanum majorana L. (sweet marjoram).[web:246][web:253]  
**Family:** Lamiaceae.  
**Parts Used:** Flowering aerial parts (leaf and flower), fresh/dried; essential oil.[web:246][web:256]

### Functional Overview

- Energetics: Warming, aromatic, slightly drying.[web:247]  
- Actions: Carminative, antispasmodic, mild expectorant, nervine, antimicrobial.[web:246][web:253][web:256]  
- Main uses: Mild **GI spasm/bloating**, simple coughs/catarrh, tension headaches, kitchen‑level respiratory spice.[web:246][web:249][web:253]

### Evidence & Pharmacology

- Traditional indications recognized in EU monograph: **mild spasmodic GI complaints (bloating, gas)** and coughs.[web:246]  
- Essential oil shows antimicrobial, antioxidant, anti‑inflammatory and anticancer activities in vitro and animal models.[web:256]  

### Dosing (Adults)

- Infusion: 1–2 g dried herb per cup, up to 3× daily for short‑term digestive/respiratory use.[web:246]  
- Topical: Ointment containing marjoram extract around nostrils for runny nose in pharmacopoeial preparations.[web:249]  

### Safety / Cautions

- Food amounts: **safe**.[web:247][web:259]  
- Medicinal short‑term: **possibly safe**; long‑term high‑dose use considered **possibly unsafe** (theoretical carcinogenicity, irritation).[web:250][web:259]  
- Pregnancy: Emmenagogue/uterine‑stimulating tradition; essential oil **not for pregnancy/lactation**, and most sources avoid internal medicinal doses in pregnancy.[web:247][web:253]  
- Children: Not recommended in medicinal doses; stick to culinary use.[web:247]  
- Surgery: Discontinue medicinal doses ≥2 weeks before surgery due to possible bleeding risk.[web:250]

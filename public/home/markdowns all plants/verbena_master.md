# VERVAIN (Verbena officinalis) – COMMON VERVAIN

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Verbena officinalis L. (Vervain, Common vervain) — Perennial herb native to Europe and naturalized widely; slender, somewhat woody stems with small lilac flowers; not the same as blue vervain (Verbena hastata), though actions overlap.[web:116][web:107][web:110]  
**Botanical Family:** Verbenaceae.[web:116]  
**Part Used:** **Aerial parts** (flowering tops, leaves, upper stems) collected in bloom.[web:116][web:110]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvest flowering tops during peak bloom; dry rapidly in shade with airflow. Fresh plant tinctures commonly used for heightened nervine activity.[web:116][web:110]  
**Quality Indicators:** Characteristic bitter, slightly aromatic taste; greenish to grey‑green dried tops with intact flowers; not dusty or brown.[web:116][web:110]  
**Storage:** Dried herb in airtight containers, cool and dark; best potency within 1 year. Tinctures stable several years in dark glass.[web:116]  
**Historical Context:** Sacred herb in European folk magic (associated with purification, prophecy, and protection), and medicinally as a **bitter nervine, hepatic, and remedy for stress, headaches, and women’s complaints**.[web:104][web:107][web:110][web:120]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Iridoid Glycosides:** Verbenalin, hastatoside — core to vervain’s nervine, sedative, anti‑inflammatory, and potential neuroprotective actions.[web:116][web:110]  
- **Triterpenes:** Oleanolic and ursolic acids — anti‑inflammatory, hepatoprotective, antimicrobial.[web:110]  
- **Flavonoids:** Luteolin, apigenin, others — antioxidant, anxiolytic, neuroprotective.[web:116][web:110]  
- **Volatile Oils & Others:** Minor constituents contributing to mild spasmolytic and aromatic effects.

**Mechanisms & Key Studies:**

- **Anxiolytic & Sedative:**  
  - Animal studies show anxiolytic effects in elevated plus maze and open field tests at extracts of 0.1–0.5 g/kg, comparable to diazepam.[web:120][web:123]  
  - Vervain increases thiopental‑induced sleep duration and exhibits anticonvulsant actions in PTZ‑induced seizure models.[web:123]

- **Neuroprotective:**  
  - In vitro, aqueous extracts significantly reduce β‑amyloid toxicity and caspase activation in cortical neurons, suggesting potential protection in Alzheimer‑type pathology.[web:116]

- **Autonomic & Nervous System:**  
  - Clinically, used by herbalists to “rebalance ANS,” particularly sympathetic overdrive; acts as a relaxing nervine with mild bitter stimulation.[web:116][web:107][web:110]

- **Hepatic / Anti‑inflammatory:**  
  - Triterpenes and flavonoids confer hepatoprotective and anti‑inflammatory effects; aligns with use for liver congestion, headaches, and PMS.[web:110]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Infusion (Dried Herb):**  
- 1–2 teaspoons (~1–3 g) dried herb per 250 ml boiling water; infuse 10–15 minutes; **1–3× daily**.[web:116][web:110]  
- Bitter taste: often combined with aromatics (mint, lemon balm).

**Tincture (1:5 40–60%):**  
- Common range: **1–5 ml, 2–3× daily** (≈20–100 drops per dose).[web:116][web:107][web:110]  
- Lower end for sensitive individuals; higher end for acute tension episodes.

**Fluid Extract:**  
- 1:1 — typically **0.5–2 ml, 1–3× daily** (if used).

**Duration:**  
- Can be used for **several months** at moderate doses; often cycled (e.g., 5 days on / 2 off) in long‑term stress patterns.  
- For migraines/PMS: expect 2–3 cycles or 4–8 weeks for full effects.

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:**  
- Strong **relaxing nervine and anxiolytic** for stress, burnout, and ANS imbalance.[web:116][web:107][web:110][web:123]  
- **Headache/migraine, PMS, and stress gut** support via liver‑nervine connection.[web:116][web:110]  
- Potential **neuroprotective** actions in β‑amyloid toxicity models.[web:116]

**Safety Overview:**  
- Generally safe at typical doses; most adverse effects are mild GI upset or sedation at high doses.[web:116][web:120][web:123]

**⚠️ CRITICAL CAUTIONS**

- **Pregnancy:**  
  - Traditionally considered **contraindicated in pregnancy** due to emmenagogue and uterine‑stimulating reputation; evidence is limited but cautious avoidance is common.[web:116][web:120]  
  - For AI app: Block or strongly caution user if pregnant or trying to conceive.

**MAJOR CAUTIONS**

- **Hypotension & Sedation:**  
  - Can mildly lower BP and cause sedation; caution if on sedatives or multiple antihypertensives.[web:116][web:120]  

- **Seizure / CNS Drugs:**  
  - Animal data suggest anticonvulsant effects; interactions with antiepileptic drugs unknown—use only under supervision.[web:123]  

- **Autoimmune or Complex Neurological Conditions:**  
  - Given neuroactive profile, professional guidance is prudent.

**Drug Interactions:**  
- Possible additive effect with **CNS depressants** (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohol).[web:120]  
- Hypotensive synergy with BP‑lowering medications.  
- No major CYP interactions well documented, but caution with polypharmacy.

# GOJI BERRY / WOLFBERRY (Lycium barbarum, L. chinense)

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Lycium barbarum L. and Lycium chinense Mill. (Goji, wolfberry, gou qi zi) — Deciduous shrubs native to China and adjacent regions; bright orange‑red berries used as food and TCM herb.[web:186][web:189][web:190]  
**Botanical Family:** Solanaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Fruit (dried berries)**.[web:187][web:189]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Berries harvested when ripe, carefully dried (traditionally sun‑dried) to preserve color and polysaccharides. Often used as dried fruit, decocted in soups/teas, or extracted.[web:186][web:189]  
**Quality Indicators:** Bright orange‑red, plump berries with sweet taste and small seeds; free from sulfur bleaching or heavy pesticide residues. Authentic species identification (L. barbarum vs L. chinense) important for consistency.[web:189][web:190]  
**Storage:** Airtight, cool/dark; protect from moisture and insects.[web:187]  
**Historical Context:** In TCM, goji is a core **blood and yin tonic**, used for over 2000 years to support **liver, kidney, eyes, vitality, and longevity**.[web:187][web:189] Modern research explores antioxidant, neuroprotective, metabolic, and immune‑modulating roles.[web:186][web:195][web:200]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Polysaccharides (LBPs):** Lycium barbarum polysaccharides — major bioactives; immunomodulatory, antioxidant, neuroprotective, hypoglycemic.[web:186][web:195][web:200]  
- **Carotenoids:** Zeaxanthin, lutein — retinal protective pigments.[web:186][web:189]  
- **Flavonoids, Phenolic Acids, Betaines, Vitamins (C, B) and minerals.**[web:186][web:195]

**Mechanisms:**

- **Antioxidant / Anti‑aging:**  
  - LBPs increase antioxidant enzymes, reduce lipid peroxidation, and protect against oxidative stress in multiple tissues.[web:186][web:195][web:200]  

- **Neuroprotective & Cognitive:**  
  - Animal and small human studies show improved neuronal survival, reduced β‑amyloid–related damage, and enhanced cognitive performance.[web:186][web:195][web:200]

- **Immunomodulation:**  
  - LBPs modulate innate and adaptive immunity, enhancing macrophage, NK, and T‑cell function in models.[web:186][web:195]

- **Metabolic:**  
  - Goji intake may improve glucose tolerance, insulin sensitivity, lipids, and body weight parameters in animals and some human studies.[web:186][web:195][web:200]

- **Vision:**  
  - Carotenoid‑rich goji supports macular pigment density and retinal health; small clinical trials suggest benefits in early AMD and general eye health.[web:186][web:195]

**Safety:**  

- Comprehensive review notes **no known toxicity** of L. barbarum in traditional texts or modern literature; decades of use as food.[web:186][web:189][web:195]  
- However, a few **warfarin interaction** case reports exist.[web:190]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. Blood & Yin Tonic for Eyes / Aging**  
Goji’s carotenoids and polysaccharides protect retinal cells from oxidative and metabolic damage, while TCM frames it as nourishing Liver‑Kidney blood/yin.[web:186][web:187][web:195][web:200]

**2. Metabolic & Liver Support**

**3. Immune & Anti‑fatigue**

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:**  

- **Eye and retinal support**.  
- **Blood and yin tonification; anti‑aging**.[web:186][web:187][web:195][web:200]  
- Metabolic, hepatic, and immune support.

**Safety Overview:**  

- Generally **very safe**; goji is widely consumed as food with no intrinsic toxicity identified.[web:186][web:189][web:195]

**⚠️ SPECIFIC CAUTION – WARFARIN**

- At least two case reports of **elevated INR/bleeding** when goji consumed with warfarin; mechanism may involve CYP or P‑gp modulation.[web:190]  
- Patients on warfarin should use goji cautiously and monitor INR.

**Other Cautions:**

- **Allergy:** Rare; possible in Solanaceae‑sensitive individuals.  
- Pregnancy/lactation: food‑level use is traditional; high‑dose extracts only with professional guidance.

**Drug Interactions:**

- **Warfarin:** Potential interaction; INR monitoring recommended.[web:190]  
- Theoretical: other narrow‑therapeutic‑index drugs if goji alters CYP/P‑gp (limited data).

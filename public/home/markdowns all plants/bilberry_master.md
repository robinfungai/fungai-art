# BILBERRY (Vaccinium myrtillus)

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Vaccinium myrtillus L. (Bilberry, European Blueberry, Whortleberry) — Small shrub native to northern and central Europe and parts of Asia; fruits are blue‑black berries with deeply pigmented flesh.[web:73][web:75]  
**Botanical Family:** Ericaceae.![web:75]  
**Part Used:** **Ripe berries** (fresh or dried); occasionally leaf for glycemic/cardiovascular support, but fruit is primary for eye/cardiometabolic use.[web:73][web:75]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Wild‑harvested or cultivated berries picked at full ripeness; used fresh, frozen, juiced, or dried; standardized extracts typically titrated to 25% anthocyanins (or similar).[web:73][web:75]  
**Quality Indicators:** Deeply pigmented blue‑black skin and flesh, intense berry aroma and flavor, no mold; standardized extracts should declare anthocyanin % and solvent system.[web:73][web:75]  
**Storage:** Fresh berries refrigerated or frozen; dried berries and extracts stored airtight, cool, and dark to preserve anthocyanins (sensitive to heat/light).[web:73][web:75]  
**Historical Context:** Traditional European herbal medicine used bilberries for **diarrhea, urinary issues, scurvy, and vision**.[web:73][web:75] World War II legends claimed pilots used bilberry jam for night vision, though rigorous trials have not confirmed benefit in healthy eyes.[web:71][web:81]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Anthocyanins (Anthocyanosides):** Major polyphenols responsible for blue/black color and many vascular/visual benefits; bilberry is among the richest natural sources.[web:73][web:75]  
- **Proanthocyanidins, Flavonols (quercetin, myricetin), Phenolic Acids:** Antioxidant, anti‑inflammatory, endothelial‑protective.[web:73][web:75]  
- **Vitamins & Minerals:** Vitamin C, manganese, etc., in supportive amounts.[web:73]

**Mechanisms:**

- **Retinoprotective & Visual:** Anthocyanins protect retina from light‑induced oxidative damage, reduce lipid peroxidation, modulate inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IL‑1β, VEGF), and may influence rhodopsin regeneration.[web:72][web:74][web:75]  
- **Vasoprotective:** Strengthen capillary walls, reduce capillary fragility, improve venous tone, and enhance microcirculation.[web:73][web:75]  
- **Antioxidant / Anti‑inflammatory:** Scavenge ROS, upregulate endogenous antioxidant defenses, and down‑regulate pro‑inflammatory mediators.[web:73][web:75][web:74]  
- **Metabolic:** Improve lipid profile and glycemic control in some studies; effects modest and context‑dependent.[web:73][web:75]

**Visual Evidence Nuances:**

- A systematic review of trials of V. myrtillus anthocyanosides for **night vision** found that rigorous RCTs in healthy subjects with normal vision did **not** support benefit, while older, weaker trials suggested positive effects.[web:71]  
- A 6‑week RCT in adults with **VDT‑related eye fatigue** using bilberry anthocyanin 43.2 mg/day improved pupillary response metrics vs placebo.[web:78]  
- A 12‑week RCT of standardized bilberry extract in VDT users also showed beneficial effects on ciliary muscle function and accommodative strain.[web:84]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. Retinoprotection & Eye Strain Support (Anthocyanins)**  
Bilberry anthocyanins protect retinal cells from visible‑light–induced damage by boosting antioxidant defenses, reducing lipid peroxidation, and lowering IL‑1β and VEGF in animal models.[web:72]  
Effect: Support for retinal resilience, reduction of eye fatigue and accommodative strain in heavy screen users, potential protection in light‑stress contexts.[web:72][web:78][web:84]  
Timeline: RCTs show benefits over 6–12 weeks of daily standardized extract.[web:78][web:84]  
Clinical significance: Eye health and VDT‑strain support, adjunctive care in patients at risk of retinal stress (not a replacement for conventional care).

**2. Microvascular & Cardiometabolic Support**  
Anthocyanins improve endothelial function, reduce oxidative stress and inflammation, and may modestly improve lipids and glucose.[web:73][web:75][web:74]  
Effect: Potential adjunct in CVD risk reduction, diabetic microvascular protection, and general cardiometabolic resilience.  
Timeline: Typically weeks to months.  
Clinical significance: “Berry polyphenol” motif in cardiometabolic and vascular protocols.

**3. GI Astringent & Antimicrobial (Traditional)**  
Dried bilberries used historically for diarrhea via astringent tannins and antimicrobial polyphenols.[web:73][web:75]  
Effect: Reduced loose stools in mild, non‑serious diarrhea when used as tea or dried berries.  
Timeline: Symptomatic relief over days.  
Clinical significance: Now overshadowed by more targeted therapies, but still relevant in gentle GI protocols.

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Food Use:**  
- Fresh or frozen berries: 50–150 g/day as part of diet.  
- Juice or concentrate: as per product, equivalent to “handfuls” of berries.

**Standardized Extracts (Eye / Vascular):**

- Common RCT ranges:  
  - 43.2 mg/day bilberry anthocyanins for 6 weeks in VDT eye fatigue.[web:78]  
  - Other eye/vascular trials: often **80–160 mg anthocyanins/day** (typical extract: 25% anthocyanins → ~320–640 mg extract).[web:73][web:74][web:75][web:84]

**Traditional Dried Berry Use:**

- 1–2 teaspoons (~3–5 g) dried berries per 250 ml water; simmer 10–20 minutes and drink 1–3× daily for GI/vascular support.[web:73][web:75]

**Duration:**  
- Eye‑strain protocols: 6–12 weeks, reassess.[web:78][web:84]  
- Cardiometabolic and chronic vascular support: months, often as part of ongoing polyphenol regimen.

**Example Protocols**

**Protocol 1: VDT Eye Strain (FOUNDATION)**  
- Standardized bilberry extract providing **40–80 mg anthocyanins/day** for 6–12 weeks.[web:78][web:84]  
- Combine with screen hygiene, lighting, and breaks.

**Protocol 2: General Eye Health / Aging**  
- 80–160 mg anthocyanins/day plus other eye allies (lutein/zeaxanthin, omega‑3s).

**Protocol 3: Microvascular / Cardiometabolic**

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:**  
- Retinal and eye‑strain support (VDT users).[web:72][web:78][web:84]  
- Microvascular and cardiometabolic antioxidant support.[web:73][web:75][web:74]  
- Traditional GI astringent use.[web:73][web:75]

**Safety:**  
- Bilberry fruit is **very safe** at food and typical supplement doses; generally well tolerated in trials with no serious adverse events.[web:73][web:75][web:78][web:84]  

**Cautions / Contraindications:**  
- **Anticoagulants / Antiplatelets:** Theoretically, high doses of polyphenols might affect platelet function, but bilberry is not strongly implicated; still use caution in very high doses with multiple vascular‑active agents.[web:75]  
- **Hypoglycemic Drugs:** Anthocyanins may modestly lower glucose; monitor in tightly controlled diabetics.[web:75]  
- Pregnancy & lactation: Food use is fine; standardized extract as “likely safe” but data limited—keep doses moderate and short‑term.[web:75]

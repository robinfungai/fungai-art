# COCA LEAF (Erythroxylum coca and related spp.)

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Erythroxylum coca Lam. and E. novogranatense — Shrubs native to Andean regions; leaves traditionally chewed or brewed as tea.[web:178][web:182]  
**Botanical Family:** Erythroxylaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Leaf** (dried), chewed, brewed, or used in traditional preparations.[web:182]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Leaves harvested multiple times a year, dried in shade or low sun; for traditional use, leaves may be mixed with alkaline ash (llipta) for improved alkaloid absorption.[web:182]  
**Quality Indicators:** Green, intact leaves; faint tea‑like aroma; absence of mold; proper drying.  
**Storage:** Dried leaves in airtight, light‑protected containers.  
**Legal Context:** Coca leaf is schedule I under the **1961 Single Convention**, with coca and cocaine tightly controlled globally; legal traditional use persists in Andean countries, but coca is generally **illegal or highly restricted** elsewhere.[web:178][web:182]

## FUNCTIONAL FRAMEWORK (TRADITIONAL USE)

**Energetics:**  
- Temperature: Mildly warming, stimulating.  
- Actions: Mild CNS stimulant, mood‑brightening, hunger‑suppressing, **altitude and fatigue support**, digestive tonic.[web:182][web:178]

**Primary Traditional Indications:**  
- **Altitude sickness (soroche):** Headache, fatigue, nausea from high altitude.[web:182]  
- **Fatigue & labor support:** Sustains energy in physical work at altitude.  
- **Digestive upset:** Nausea, sluggish digestion, stomachache.  
- **Mild pain and mood support.**  

**Core Principle:**  
“Whole coca leaf is a **mild stimulant and adaptogen‑like tonic** for high altitude and heavy labor, distinct in dose and risk from isolated **cocaine**, but constrained by global drug policy.”[web:178][web:182]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Tropane Alkaloids:** Primarily **cocaine**, plus cinnamoylcocaine, truxillines, etc., at low concentrations in the whole leaf (~0.5–1% cocaine by dry weight, varying).[web:178]  
- **Other:** Flavonoids, vitamins, minerals (notably calcium), and nutrients; coca tea has modest alkaloid amounts.

**Mechanisms (Whole Leaf vs. Cocaine):**

- **Cocaine (isolated drug):**  
  - Potent **monoamine reuptake inhibitor** (DAT, SERT, NET) → strong psychostimulant, euphoriant, addictive, cardiotoxic.  

- **Coca leaf (chewed/tea):**  
  - Slower absorption, lower alkaloid dosing, presence of other constituents modulate effect; traditional patterns show **mild stimulation, hunger suppression, and improved altitude tolerance** without the extreme toxicity profile of refined cocaine.[web:178][web:182]  
  - WHO ECDD review notes low toxicity and weak dependence liability when used traditional‑style; however, evidence is still limited.[web:178]

## KEY MECHANISMS & USES (TRADITIONAL CONTEXT)

**1. Altitude Sickness & Labor Endurance**

- Chewed leaves or coca tea used to reduce soroche symptoms (headache, fatigue, nausea) and sustain work at high altitude.[web:182]  
Effect: Subjective increased energy, reduced altitude discomfort, enhanced oxygen utilization tolerance (mechanisms multifactorial).

**2. Mild Stimulant & Mood Support**

- Provides gentle wakefulness, mood lift, decreased hunger, and improved tolerance of long days.

**3. Digestive & Analgesic**

- Traditional uses include relief of stomachache, colic, and mild pains.[web:182]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION (TRADITIONAL)

**Chewing (“Acullico”):**

- Small handful of dried leaves placed in cheek; alkaline ash added; chewed and held for extended absorption. Frequency varies among users.

**Tea (Mate de Coca):**

- 1–2 g leaves infused in hot water; consumed as tea, often multiple cups/day at altitude.[web:182]

**Standardized Extracts:**

- Medical coca extracts (e.g., decocainized) used historically for appetite suppression and fatigue; not widely available now.

## SAFETY, LEGALITY & ETHICS

**Toxicology & Dependence (Whole Leaf):**

- WHO ECDD critical review notes **low toxicity** and **weak evidence of dependence** potential of traditional leaf chewing; major social/health harms are associated with **refined cocaine**, not leaf chewing.[web:178]  
- Acute side effects at typical traditional doses are mild: insomnia, slight tachycardia, appetite suppression.  

**Legal Status:**

- Coca leaf is currently listed under **Schedule I of the 1961 Single Convention**, with strong international control; outside Andean countries, possession and use of coca leaf is generally illegal.[web:178][web:182]  
- Legitimate traditional and cultural uses are recognized but often stigmatized due to association with cocaine trade.[web:182]

**Ethical / Sustainability Aspects:**

- Global demand for cocaine has driven **deforestation, violence, and disruption** in coca‑growing regions, overshadowing traditional cultural relationships with the plant.[web:178][web:182]  
- Non‑local, non‑traditional demand for coca leaf risks feeding illicit supply chains and cultural appropriation.

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & CAUTIONS

**Medical cautions (where coca tea/leaf is legal):**

- **Cardiovascular disease / Hypertension:**  
  - Mild stimulant and vasoconstrictor effects can exacerbate BP or ischemia.  

- **Anxiety / Insomnia:**  
  - Stimulant may worsen symptoms.  

- **Pregnancy & Lactation:**  
  - Limited safety data; as a tropane‑alkaloid source, generally avoided.  

- **Substance Use Disorders:**  
  - Although dependence risk of leaf is low compared to cocaine, use in people with stimulant addiction histories is controversial.

**Drug Interactions:**

- **Stimulants:** Additive effects with caffeine, amphetamines, etc.  
- **MAOIs & certain antidepressants:** Theoretically enhanced monoamine elevations.  
- **Cardiac meds:** Unpredictable changes in HR/BP.

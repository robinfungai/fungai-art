# PINE NEEDLE (Pinus spp.) – PINE NEEDLE TEA

## Master Monograph: Respiratory / Antiviral‑Adjunct Herb (Data Limited)

**Primary Species:** Commonly Pinus sylvestris, P. strobus, and other non‑toxic pine species; some conifers (e.g., yew, ponderosa pine) are **toxic and not suitable**.[web:223][web:227]  
**Botanical Family:** Pinaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Needles** (fresh or dried); sometimes twigs.  

---

## FUNCTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**Traditional Uses:**  

- Respiratory support (colds, flu), vitamin C source, antimicrobial inhalations.[web:227]  

**Energetics:**  
- Temperature: Warming, aromatic.  
- Actions: Mild expectorant, antimicrobial, antioxidant, **shikimic‑acid–containing antiviral adjunct**.[web:223][web:227]

**Core Principle:**  
“Pine needle tea is a **mild respiratory and antioxidant infusion** containing shikimic acid and other compounds with in vitro antiviral and anti‑inflammatory potential, but species choice and pregnancy safety are critical.”[web:223][web:227]

---

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Shikimic Acid:** Precursor for oseltamivir (Tamiflu); present in pine needles in notable amounts (though less than star anise).[web:223][web:227]  
- **Terpenes:** α‑pinene, β‑pinene, limonene, bornyl acetate.  
- Vitamin C, flavonoids.

**Mechanisms (Proposed):**

- **Antiviral:**  
  - Shikimic acid and terpenes show inhibitory effects on certain viruses in vitro; speculation around influenza; human data limited.[web:223][web:227]

- **Anti‑inflammatory:**  
  - Shikimic acid exhibits anti‑inflammatory properties in models; may reduce chronic inflammation risk.[web:223]

- **Respiratory Support:**  
  - Aromatic vapors act as mild decongestants/expectorants.

---

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Tea:**

- 1–2 tsp chopped needles per cup hot (not boiling) water; steep 10–20 minutes; 1–3 cups/day in acute phases.[web:223][web:227]  

**Inhalation (Steam):**

- Needles in hot water for inhaled vapors.

**Duration:**

- Typically short‑term (days to a few weeks) around respiratory infections.

---

## SAFETY, SPECIES & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Species Safety:**  

- **Safe-ish pines:** Eastern white pine (P. strobus), Scots pine (P. sylvestris), some other Pinus species.  
- **Avoid:** Ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa), lodgepole, Monterey cypress, and especially **yew (Taxus spp.)**, which is **highly toxic** and not a pine.[web:227]

**Pregnancy & Fertility:**  

- Pine needles contain phytoestrogens; one animal study on ponderosa pine needles linked them to **abortions in cattle**, raising concern.[web:227]  
- Many herbalists and sources advise **avoiding pine needle tea in pregnancy and when trying to conceive**.[web:227]

**Other Cautions:**

- **Allergy/asthma:** Terpene‑rich vapor can trigger sensitive lungs.  
- **Kidney disease:** High terpene intake may be irritating; keep doses modest.

**Drug Interactions:**  

- None well documented; theoretical with other nephrotoxic or strong CNS‑stimulating agents.

## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:** Urtica dioica L. (Stinging Nettle, Common Nettle) — Europe, Asia and North America widespread; one of the most important nutritive tonic herbs in Western herbalism; leaf primary medicinal use for mineral nourishment, allergy support and gentle diuresis.

**Botanical Family:** Urticaceae

**Part Used:** **Leaf only** for this monograph — harvested spring to early summer before flowering for best nutritive and tonic quality. Root is a distinct medicine with different actions (especially male urinary/prostate support) and should not be confused with leaf.

**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvest young aerial leaf in gloves before seed set when color is vibrant and mineral density is high. Dry gently with airflow and low heat to preserve chlorophyll, flavonoids and water-soluble minerals. Fresh plant stings due to formic acid, histamine and mechanical hairs; drying, cooking or blending neutralizes sting.

**Quality Indicators:** Deep vibrant green color, clean herbaceous aroma, low stem content, no yellowing or browning, spring or early summer harvest preferred. Potency indicated by rich green color and strong infusion mineral taste.

**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark, low humidity. Best within 12–24 months for maximal green vitality and mineral integrity.

**Historical Context:** Longstanding food-medicine herb across Europe and beyond. Used traditionally as spring tonic, blood builder, kidney-supportive herb, allergy ally, galactagogue and postpartum nutritive. Modern research supports anti-inflammatory, antihistamine, diuretic and nutritive actions, though many of its deepest benefits are traditional, cumulative and food-like rather than pharmaceutical in expression.

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Dosage Range:**

**Long-Steep Infusion (Traditional — OPTIMAL):** 2–4 g dried leaf per cup, steep 10–15 minutes minimum; for deeper nutritive work, 30–60 g per quart steeped several hours or overnight. This is the classic mineral-building preparation.

**Tea / Short Infusion (Light Daily Use):** 1–2 tsp dried leaf per cup, steep 10 minutes, 1–3× daily. Gentler and more casual than deep nutritive infusion.

## ⚠️ CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:** GRADE A — extremely safe, food-like, highly tolerable for most people when used appropriately.

**Minor Cautions:**

**Diuretic Medications:** May have additive diuretic effects; monitor hydration and electrolytes, especially in fragile or medicated individuals.

**Kidney Disease / Renal Instability:** Even gentle diuresis may require modification in advanced renal conditions; coordinate with practitioner.

**Warfarin / Anticoagulants (Large Daily Doses):** Vitamin K content may matter in large sustained quantities; monitor INR consistency rather than assume incompatibility.

**Iron Supplement Timing:** Very mild tannin-related interference is possible but usually minimal; separate from standalone iron supplements by 1–2 hours if using therapeutically.

**Pregnancy:** Generally regarded as safe in moderate leaf use, especially in second and third trimester traditional practice, though conservative first-trimester caution is reasonable when uncertainty exists.

**Herb-to-Herb Interactions:**

**Oatstraw and Lucerne / Alfalfa (Synergistic — Nutritive):** Classic mineral rebuilding trinity.

**Raspberry Leaf (Synergistic — Pregnancy/Postpartum):** Excellent uterine-tonic plus mineral-nutritive pairing.

**Dandelion Leaf (Synergistic — Fluid / Mineral):** Combined green mineral diuretic support.

**Chamomile (Synergistic — Allergy / Inflammation):** Histamine and soothing anti-inflammatory complement.

**Caution With Multiple Diuretic Herbs:** Can overemphasize fluid movement in depleted clients if not balanced with nourishment.

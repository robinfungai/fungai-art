# PEPPERMINT (Mentha × piperita) – LEAF & OIL

## Master Monograph

**Primary Species:** Mentha × piperita L. (hybrid of M. aquatica × M. spicata).[web:257]  
**Family:** Lamiaceae.  
**Parts Used:** Leaf (tea, extract) and **essential oil** (enteric‑coated capsules, topical).[web:257][web:251]

### Functional Overview

- Energetics: Cooling, aromatic, drying.  
- Actions: Carminative, **antispasmodic**, mild analgesic, decongestant.[web:251][web:257]  
- Main uses: **IBS and functional gut pain**, gas/bloating, tension headaches, nasal congestion.

### Evidence & Pharmacology

- Key constituent: **L‑menthol**, plus menthone and other monoterpenes.[web:251][web:257]  
- Mechanism: Menthol blocks **smooth‑muscle calcium channels**, giving GI antispasmodic effects.[web:251]  
- Systematic reviews and RCTs (incl. IBgard® and other enteric‑coated oils) show peppermint oil significantly improves global IBS symptoms and abdominal pain vs placebo, though evidence quality is low–moderate and adverse events higher than placebo.[web:251][web:254][web:257][web:260]

### Dosing (Adults)

- Enteric‑coated peppermint oil: **0.08–0.2 ml oil**, 2–3 (up to 4)× daily before meals in IBS trials and EU monograph.[web:257][web:251][web:254]  
- Tea (leaf): 1.5–3 g dried leaf per cup, up to 3× daily for mild dyspepsia/colds.  

### Safety / Cautions

- Common AEs with PO: Heartburn, anal burning, peppermint reflux, nausea.[web:251][web:254]  
- **Contraindications:**  
  - Bile duct obstruction, severe liver disease, gallstones (for oil).[web:257]  
  - **Severe GERD/hiatal hernia** – may worsen reflux.  
  - Children <8 with oil around face/nostrils (risk of laryngospasm/bronchospasm).  
- Pregnancy/lactation: Culinary use fine; medicinal oil use only short‑term and cautiously (data limited).  
- Drug interactions: Menthol may inhibit certain CYPs in vitro, but clinically significant interactions at standard doses are not well documented.

## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Cetraria islandica (L.) Acharius s.l. (Iceland Moss, Lichen islandicus) — A lichen (symbiosis of fungus + alga) native to arctic and subarctic regions; grows on rocks/soil in Iceland, Scandinavia, alpine Europe.[web:51][web:53][web:65]

**Botanical Family:**  
Parmeliaceae (lichenized ascomycetes).[web:53]

**Part Used:**  
Lichen thallus (whole dried “moss”).[web:53][web:60]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Collected from clean areas (away from pollution), washed and dried at low temperatures; may be briefly macerated/soaked to remove some bitter lichen acids depending on intended use.[web:52][web:60]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Dried, curled thalli, grey-green to brownish with whitish underside; slightly bitter and mucilaginous when soaked; free from excessive soil/debris.[web:53][web:60]

**Storage:**  
Dry, airtight, protected from light; retains demulcent activity for several years.[web:53]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**  

**Primary Bioactives:**  
- **Polysaccharides/mucilage (lichenan, isolichenan):** Strong demulcent and immunomodulatory effects; soothe irritated mucosa.[web:52][web:53]  
- **Lichen acids (cetraric acid, fumarprotocetraric acid):** Antimicrobial, bitter, possibly anti-inflammatory; contribute to gastric and respiratory effects.[web:52][web:60]  

**Mechanisms:**  
- **Demulcent:** Forms protective mucous-like layer on oral, pharyngeal, and gastric mucosa; reduces irritation and cough reflex.[web:52][web:53][web:60]  
- **Antimicrobial:** Lichen acids show antibiotic-like activity against various bacteria and possibly fungi; used in cough syrups/lozenges.[web:52][web:60]  
- **Immunomodulatory:** Polysaccharides stimulate aspects of innate immunity in experimental models.[web:52]  
- **Bitter Tonic:** Unique combination of demulcent and bitter tones digestion and appetite while soothing at the same time.[web:60]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. Demulcent for Dry Cough / Throat Irritation (PRIMARY)**  
Iceland moss coats and soothes mucosa in mouth, throat, and upper airways, reducing the urge to cough and easing dryness and tickle.[web:52][web:53][web:65]  
Effect: Relieves dry, non-productive cough, hoarseness, sore throat, and irritation from talking/singing.  

**2. Gastroprotective & Anti-Irritant**  
Demulcent polysaccharides and lichen acids protect and soothe gastric mucosa; used for gastritis, ulcers, reflux, and general stomach sensitivity.[web:52][web:55][web:60]  
Effect: Reduced pain, burning, and nausea; improved appetite and digestion in chronic irritation.

**3. Mild Antimicrobial & Immune-Modulating**  
Antibiotic-like lichen acids plus immunostimulant polysaccharides support host defense in respiratory and GI infections.[web:52][web:60]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Traditional Soak + Decoction (to balance bitterness/demulcency):**  
- 1–2 tsp (2–4 g) cut lichen soaked briefly in cold water (to reduce very strong bitterness if needed), then simmered gently 10–20 minutes in 250 ml water; drink 2–4× daily.[web:52][web:53]

**Cold Maceration (Max Demulcent):**  
- 1–2 tbsp lichen in 500 ml cold water, soak several hours or overnight, stir and strain; drink cool in divided doses.

**Syrups / Lozenges:**  
- Standardized Iceland moss syrups and lozenges are widely used in Europe for dry cough and throat irritation according to EMA traditional monograph.[web:53][web:65]

**Duration:**  
- Safe for days–weeks for acute respiratory/GI issues; can be used longer term as food-like tonic in chronic mucosal sensitivity.[web:53][web:52]

**Best Preparations & Protocols:**  

**Protocol 1: Dry, Tickle Cough / Sore Throat**  
- Iceland moss tea or syrup 3–5× daily in small sips.  
- Combine with thyme (for expectorant/antimicrobial), licorice (if BP-safe), and marshmallow root.

**Protocol 2: Gastritis / Ulcer / Reflux**  
- Cold maceration or cooled decoction before meals, 2–3× daily.  
- Combine with DGL licorice, marshmallow, and plantain leaf.

**Protocol 3: Elderly / Chronic Respiratory Fragility**  
- Regular low-dose decoction or syrup to protect fragile mucosa in chronic cough/bronchitis.[web:60]

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:**  
GRADE A- — Generally very safe; used as food in traditional diets and as registered traditional herbal medicine in EU.[web:53][web:65]

**Contraindications / Cautions:**  
- **Known lichen allergy:** Rare; discontinue if rash or GI upset.  
- **Severe autoimmune conditions:** Theoretical caution due to immunostimulant polysaccharides, though clinical significance unclear.

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**  
- None well documented; risk of clinically significant interactions considered low at typical doses.[web:53][web:65]

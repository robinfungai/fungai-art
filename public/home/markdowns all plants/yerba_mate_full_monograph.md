**Primary Species:** Ilex paraguariensis A.St.-Hil. **Botanical Family:** Aquifoliaceae (holly family—same as Guayusa)

**Part Used:** Ilex paraguariensis folium (leaves)—**Dried leaves optimal** (concentrate caffeine + polyphenols); fresh leaves also potent; whole leaf preferred (higher bioactivity). Leaf = only medicinal part.

**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvested year-round (evergreen); dried/roasted (traditional roasting creates flavor + compounds); whole leaves stored. Fresh leaf infusion most potent (preserves volatiles).

**Quality Indicators:** Green/brown color (roasting level—darker = more roasted); distinctive herbal, slightly smoky aroma (roasting); bitter, astringent taste (caffeine + polyphenol marker). Potency indicated by color + bitterness intensity.

**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark; maintains potency 12-18 months. Proper storage important (caffeine + polyphenols degrade).

**Historical Context:** South American indigenous (Guaraní) use 1000+ years (hunting energy, social ritual); Spanish colonial adoption; contemporary South American staple (Argentina, Paraguay, Brazil—cultural symbol); modern research (100+ studies on caffeine, energy, cognitive, antioxidant, metabolism); increasingly popular Western discovery (2000s onward).

**Heart Arrhythmia (CAUTION—STIMULATING):** Caffeine may trigger arrhythmias in susceptible. **Monitor; generally safer than higher-caffeine sources (consult cardiologist if concern).**

**Insomnia (AFTERNOON ONLY—MILD EVENING RISK):** Less sleep-disruptive than coffee/Guayusa (lower caffeine). **Morning-afternoon safe; evening may disrupt (caution).**

**Pregnancy (CAUTION—Caffeine):** Moderate caffeine; safety limited (though gentler than high-caffeine sources). **Consult prenatal provider; likely minimize.**

**Breastfeeding (CAUTION—Caffeine Transfer):** Caffeine transfers to breast milk; moderate amount. **Consult provider; generally safe (lower than coffee) but monitor infant response.**

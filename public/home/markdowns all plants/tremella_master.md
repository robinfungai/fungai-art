# TREMELLA (Tremella fuciformis) – SNOW FUNGUS / WHITE WOOD EAR

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Tremella fuciformis Berk (Snow Fungus, White Jelly Fungus, White Wood Ear, Bai Mu Er / Yin Er) — Gelatinous, white to translucent parasitic jelly fungus, traditionally cultivated on hardwood logs and associated fungi (e.g., Annulohypoxylon).[web:41][web:49][web:52] Widely used as culinary fungus and tonic food in China, Japan, and East Asia.  
**Fungal Family:** Tremellaceae.  
**Part Used:** Entire **fruiting body** (dried and rehydrated) and standardized **polysaccharide extracts**.[web:41][web:45][web:48]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Fruiting bodies harvested at full expansion; washed, then dried at low temperature to preserve polysaccharides. For cuisine, dried tremella is rehydrated and cooked in soups and desserts; for supplements, hot‑water extraction yields polysaccharide‑rich powders.[web:41][web:44][web:45]  
**Quality Indicators:** Clean, pale white to light yellow, translucent fronds; elastic, jelly‑like texture when rehydrated; neutral to mildly mushroom‑sweet aroma; no musty odors. Polysaccharide powders should be off‑white to light yellow and easily dispersible.[web:41][web:44]  
**Storage:** Dried tremella stored airtight, cool, and dry; maintains quality 18–24 months. Extract powders kept in airtight containers away from heat, moisture, and light.[web:44]  
**Historical Context:** Revered in Traditional Chinese Medicine and imperial cuisine as a **beauty and longevity mushroom**, especially for women; famous for moistening yin, nourishing lungs and stomach, and promoting radiant skin and deep nourishment after illness or depletion.[web:41][web:49][web:52][web:55]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds (Key Constituents):**

- **Polysaccharides (Tremella fuciformis Polysaccharides – TFP / TFPS):**  
  High‑molecular‑weight, branched glucuronoxylomannans and related heteropolysaccharides are the primary bioactive fraction.[web:45][web:48][web:50][web:51]  
  Actions: **Immunomodulatory, antioxidant, anti‑aging, neuroprotective, hypoglycemic, hypolipidemic, radioprotective, skin‑hydrating, anti‑inflammatory.**[web:45][web:48][web:50][web:53]

- **Minor Components:**  
  Mannose, fucose, xylose and other monosaccharides in side chains; trace proteins, minerals, vitamins, and antioxidant compounds; beta‑glucan‑like structures contributing to immune modulation.[web:45][web:48][web:51][web:47]

**Mechanisms (Modern Research):**

- **Immunomodulation / BRM (Biological Response Modulator):**  
  TF polysaccharides enhance or balance immune function (NK cells, macrophages, T/B cells), and have been developed into **enteric‑coated capsules** (approved in China) to treat leukopenia induced by chemotherapy/radiotherapy and as adjuvant therapy for chronic hepatitis and infections.[web:45][web:48][web:51]

- **Anti‑oxidation / Anti‑aging:**  
  TF polysaccharides significantly reduce ROS, protect cells from oxidative injury, and delay cellular aging; they protect skin fibroblasts from oxidative stress and support collagen and matrix integrity.[web:45][web:48][web:53]  

- **Neuroprotection & Cognitive Support:**  
  Animal and cell studies show neurotrophic effects (promoting neurite outgrowth) and protection of neurons; a double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial in individuals with subjective cognitive impairment found TF at 600–1200 mg/day improved short‑term memory and executive function without increasing adverse events.[web:43][web:54]  

- **Hypoglycemic / Hypolipidemic:**  
  TF polysaccharides lower blood glucose, improve insulin sensitivity, and reduce lipids in preclinical and clinical contexts; they modulate lipid metabolism and may improve metabolic profile.[web:45][web:48][web:51][web:50]

- **Skin Hydration & Barrier:**  
  TF polysaccharides form a hydrating film, bind large amounts of water (often compared to or exceeding hyaluronic acid), improve skin moisture, texture, and elasticity, and support wound healing and barrier function.[web:47][web:53]  

- **Anti‑inflammatory & Microbiota‑Modulating:**  
  TFPS inhibit production of pro‑inflammatory cytokines and can ameliorate atopic dermatitis in mice via immune modulation and gut microbiota changes.[web:45][web:50][web:48][web:47]

- **Radioprotective & Hematopoietic Support:**  
  TF polysaccharides increase survival in irradiated animals, protect bone marrow, and were developed clinically to alleviate leukopenia in cancer patients receiving chemo/radiotherapy.[web:44][web:45][web:48]

TF polysaccharides act as biological response modifiers, enhancing bone marrow function and immune cell counts while balancing immune responses.[web:45][web:48][web:51]  
Effect: Increased WBC counts in leukopenia, improved resilience during cancer therapy, and support in chronic infections and chronic hepatitis as adjuvant therapy.[web:45][web:48][web:51]  
Timeline: In clinical use, often over several weeks to months in capsule form.  
Clinical significance: Adjunct in chemo/radiation protocols (under oncology supervision), chronic hepatitis, chronic infections, and immune weakness.

TF extracts show neurotrophic and neuroprotective effects in cell and animal models, and human trial data indicate improved subjective memory and executive function in older adults with SCI.[web:43][web:54]  
Effect: Potential support for age‑related cognitive decline, “brain fog,” and stress‑related cognitive fatigue.  
Timeline: 8‑week clinical trial showed measurable cognitive benefits without increased adverse events.[web:43]  
Clinical significance: Nutritional cognitive support mushroom; useful in longevity and brain health blends.

**4. Metabolic & Cardiometabolic Support (Hypoglycemic / Hypolipidemic / Anti‑inflammatory)**  
TFPS reduce blood glucose, lipids, and inflammatory markers in preclinical and some clinical settings, likely via improved insulin signaling, modulation of lipid metabolism, and anti‑inflammatory effects.[web:45][web:48][web:51][web:50]  
Effect: Better glycemic control, improved lipid profile, reduced systemic inflammation, and protection of liver and cardiovascular tissues.  
Timeline: Weeks to months of continuous use.  
Clinical significance: Adjunct in metabolic syndrome, T2DM, dyslipidemia, and chronic inflammatory conditions (within a comprehensive protocol).

**5. Dermatological & Atopic Dermatitis Modulation**  
TF polysaccharides improve skin barrier, reduce oxidative and inflammatory damage, and modulate gut‑immune axis in atopic dermatitis models.[web:50][web:53][web:47]  
Effect: Reduced dermatitis severity, better skin hydration, improved barrier function and resilience.  
Timeline: Animal and preliminary human data suggest weeks of consistent intake and/or topical use.  
Clinical significance: Beauty‑from‑within formulas, atopic dermatitis adjunct, eczema/dry skin support, wound healing support.

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Culinary & Traditional Use:**  
- **Dried Fruiting Body (Whole Tremella):**  
  Typically 3–10 g dried tremella rehydrated and cooked in soups, congees, or sweet dessert broths with goji berries, jujube, etc., taken daily or several times per week as a tonic food.[web:41][web:52][web:55]

**Supplement / Extract Use:**

- **Standardized Polysaccharide Extracts (Internal):**  
  - Common general health dose: **1–3 g/day** of polysaccharide‑rich extract (often equivalent to ~5–15 g crude mushroom).[web:44][web:45][web:48]  
  - Higher supportive range: **3–6 g/day** polysaccharide extract for intensive immune, radiotherapy, or metabolic support (under professional guidance).[web:44][web:45][web:48]

- **Capsules / Tablets:**  
  Doses vary; many products deliver 500–1000 mg extract per capsule, taken 1–3× daily to reach target grams.

- **Tincture:**  
  Less common, as polysaccharides are water‑soluble; if used, dual extraction or glycerites can be employed. For polysaccharide emphasis, decoction or hot‑water extract powder is preferred.

**Preparation Forms:**

- **Decoction / Soup:** Simmer rehydrated tremella with water (and optionally goji, jujube, ginger, etc.) for 30–90 minutes until gelatinous and soft; consume as soup or dessert.[web:41][web:52][web:55]  
- **Powder / Extract:** Stir into warm beverages, smoothies, or added to formulations for skin, brain, or immune health.  
- **Topical / Cosmetic:** TF polysaccharides are incorporated into serums, creams, and masks as hydrating, anti‑aging actives.[web:47][web:53]

**Duration:**  
- As tonic food: Safe and appropriate for long‑term, daily or frequent use.[web:41]  
- As concentrated extract: Commonly used in **8–12+ week** blocks, sometimes longer under supervision (especially in oncology/metabolic contexts).[web:44][web:45][web:48]

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Key Benefits (Summary):**

- **Yin & Fluid Nourisher:** Moistens Lung and Stomach, eases dry cough, dryness, and deficiency heat patterns.[web:41][web:46][web:49][web:52][web:55]  
- **Skin Hydration & Anti‑aging:** Potent hydrating polysaccharides improve skin moisture, elasticity, and protect against oxidative and photoaging damage.[web:47][web:53]  
- **Immune Modulation & Leukopenia Support:** Clinically utilized polysaccharide capsules for leukopenia in cancer therapy; broad immunomodulatory and anti‑tumor properties.[web:45][web:48][web:51][web:44]  
- **Neuroprotection & Cognitive Support:** Neurotrophic, neuroprotective, and cognition‑supporting effects in preclinical and human SCI trial.[web:43][web:54]  
- **Metabolic & Anti‑inflammatory:** Hypoglycemic, hypolipidemic, and anti‑inflammatory effects, including improvements in models of atopic dermatitis via immune and microbiota modulation.[web:45][web:48][web:50]

**Safety Profile:**

- Generally regarded as **very safe**, widely consumed as food with **no significant toxicity** reported in humans at culinary or commonly used supplement doses.[web:41][web:44][web:45][web:48]  

**⚠️ CRITICAL CAUTIONS**

- **Pregnancy & Breastfeeding:**  
  Although no clear toxicity has been reported, data are **insufficient**, so many sources recommend **avoiding high‑dose supplements** during pregnancy or lactation; food‑level use in traditional recipes is generally considered acceptable.[web:41][web:44][web:48]

- **Autoimmune Conditions / Immune Modulation:**  
  As an immunomodulator, theoretical caution in patients with autoimmune disease or on immunosuppressive therapy; most data indicate modulation rather than overstimulation, but clinician oversight is recommended.[web:45][web:48][web:50]

- **Bleeding / Anticoagulation (Theoretical):**  
  Some sources note mild anticoagulant or platelet‑modulating activity, suggesting caution in patients on strong anticoagulants or with bleeding disorders, though robust clinical data are limited.[web:44][web:48]

**Contraindications:**  
- No strong absolute contraindications identified in healthy adults; main caution is **lack of data in pregnancy/lactation** and theoretical concerns in specific complex conditions.[web:41][web:44][web:48]

**Drug Interactions:**  
- **None firmly established.**  
- Theoretical interactions: immunosuppressive drugs, anticoagulants/antiplatelets, and hypoglycemic medications due to its immune and metabolic actions; monitor or consult if combining.[web:44][web:45][web:48]

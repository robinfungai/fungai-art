# VITEX (Vitex agnus-castus) – CHASTE TREE BERRY

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Vitex agnus-castus L. (Chaste tree, Chasteberry, Monk’s pepper) — Deciduous shrub native to the Mediterranean and Western Asia; aromatic leaves and spikes of purple flowers followed by small, peppery berries.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  
**Botanical Family:** Lamiaceae (formerly Verbenaceae).  
**Part Used:** **Dried fruits (berries)**.[web:148][web:151]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Berries collected when fully mature and dried; extracts and extracts prepared from dried fruit, often hydroalcoholic, sometimes standardized.[web:148]  
**Quality Indicators:** Brownish, pepper‑like fruits with strong aromatic, slightly bitter flavor; free of mold. Extracts should state drug:extract ratio and solvent.[web:148]  
**Storage:** Dried berries in airtight glass, cool/dark; stable 1–2 years. Extracts per manufacturer’s guidance.[web:148]  
**Historical Context:** Traditional use since antiquity for **menstrual irregularities, PMS, “uterine conditions,” and as an anaphrodisiac** (monastic use).[web:148][web:151] Modern evidence focuses on **luteal phase defects, PMS/PMDD, hyperprolactinemia, and PCOS‑related ovulatory issues.**[web:145][web:148][web:151]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Diterpenes:** e.g., rotundifuran, which act as **dopamine D2 receptor agonists** at the pituitary.[web:145][web:148]  
- **Flavonoids:** Casticin, vitexin, etc. – antioxidant and possibly modulate receptor signaling.[web:148]  
- **Essential Oil & Iridoids:** Minor roles.

**Mechanisms:**

- **Pituitary Dopaminergic Action & Prolactin Modulation:**  
  Vitex diterpenes bind D2 receptors, reducing prolactin secretion from the anterior pituitary. This normalizes prolactin levels when elevated, leading to improved luteal phase progesterone and cycle regularity.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  

- **Dose‑Dependent Endocrine Effects:**  
  - **Low doses:** May **increase prolactin and lower estrogen**, possibly via different pathway; can act as galactagogue in low doses (traditional).[web:145]  
  - **Higher doses:** Decrease prolactin; increase luteal progesterone and sometimes estradiol, improving fertility and PMS.[web:145][web:148]  

- **Sex Hormone Balance & PCOS:**  
  Animal and clinical data show vitex can lower elevated testosterone and normalize cycles in PCOS.[web:148]

**Evidence Summaries:**

- **Hyperprolactinemia & Luteal Defect:**  
  - RCTs with 20–40 mg/day Vitex in women with cyclic mastalgia or luteal phase defect show reduced prolactin and normalized luteal function.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  
  - In a trial with elevated prolactin, 20 mg Vitex normalized prolactin and menstrual cycles after 3 months, with increased luteal estradiol.[web:148][web:151]

- **PMS/PMDD:**  
  - Multiple RCTs show significant reduction in PMS symptoms (breast tenderness, irritability, mood swings, headaches) with Vitex vs placebo, with up to 93% of women reporting improvement in some studies.[web:151]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. Prolactin Reduction & Luteal Phase Support**  
By stimulating D2 receptors, Vitex reduces elevated prolactin, which unblocks GnRH pulsatility and supports corpus luteum progesterone production.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  
Effect: Normalization of luteal phase length, improved progesterone production, restored ovulation in some amenorrhea/oligomenorrhea cases, especially when stress or hyperprolactinemia are factors.[web:145][web:148]  
Timeline: Typically **3 months** for full effect; some improvement within 1 cycle.

**2. PMS & PMDD Symptom Reduction**  
Through prolactin normalization and improved luteal hormone balance, Vitex reduces breast tenderness, fluid retention, irritability, and mood swings.[web:151][web:148]  
Effect: Significant PMS symptom improvement vs placebo in multiple trials; some data for PMDD as well.  
Timeline: 3 cycles of continuous use standard in trials.[web:151]

**3. PCOS & Hyperandrogenism (Adjunct)**  
Animal and clinical data indicate Vitex can lower elevated testosterone and improve cycles in PCOS, possibly via PRL reduction and HPO axis normalization.[web:148]  
Effect: More regular cycles, improved fertility potential, reduction in some hyperandrogenic symptoms.  
Timeline: 3–6 months.

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Standardized Extracts (Most RCTs):**

- Common daily doses: **20–40 mg of dry extract** (usually equivalent to ~160–240 mg dried fruit) once daily.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  
- Many commercial products provide 20 mg or 40 mg standardized extract per capsule.

**Traditional Whole Berry:**

- 500–1000 mg dried berries once daily (capsule), or  
- Extract: 1:5 — **1–5 ml once daily**, usually in the morning.[web:148]

**Dosing Principles:**

- Take once daily in the **morning** (pituitary action) away from other dopamine‑related drugs.  
- For **PMS/PMDD/cycle issues**, use **continuously** through cycles, not just luteal phase (unless specific protocol chosen).  
- Minimum treatment duration: **3 months**; may continue 6–12 months with monitoring.

**Example Protocols**

**Protocol 1: PMS / PMDD (FOUNDATION)**  
- 20–40 mg/day standardized extract for at least 3 consecutive cycles.[web:151]  
- Combine with magnesium, B6, and lifestyle interventions.

**Protocol 2: Hyperprolactinemia with Luteal Defect**  
- 20 mg/day standardized extract for 3–6 months, monitoring prolactin and cycle changes.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  
- Coordinate with endocrinology if on dopamine agonists.

**Protocol 3: PCOS‑Related Cycle Irregularity (ADJUNCT)**  
- 20–40 mg/day standardized Vitex with metabolic interventions (diet, exercise, berberine/inusitol as appropriate)[web:148].  
- Track cycles, ovulation (BBT, LH tests).

**Protocol 4: Perimenopausal Cyclic Mastalgia & PMS**  
- 20–40 mg/day to ease breast tenderness and mood swings in perimenopausal cycles.

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:**  
- **PMS/PMDD relief:** Mood, mastalgia, headaches, bloating.[web:151]  
- **Hyperprolactinemia & luteal defect:** Lower prolactin, improved progesterone, normalized cycles.[web:145][web:148][web:151]  
- **PCOS & hormone imbalance:** Adjunct for cycles and hyperandrogenism.[web:148]

**Safety Overview:**  

- Generally well tolerated; side effects mild and infrequent: nausea, headache, mild skin reactions, menstrual changes.[web:148][web:151]  

**⚠️ CRITICAL CONTRAINDICATIONS**

- **Pregnancy:**  
  - Not recommended once pregnancy is established; theoretical risk via hormonal modulation; many clinicians stop Vitex after conception (or at first missed period) unless specialist indicates otherwise.[web:148][web:151]

- **Hormone‑Sensitive Cancers (Breast, Uterine, etc.):**  
  - Due to modulation of estrogen/progesterone balance and prolactin, avoid in active hormone‑sensitive cancers unless oncology/endocrine team approves.[web:148]

- **Dopamine Agonists/Antagonists:**  
  - Vitex may interfere with drugs like bromocriptine, cabergoline, levodopa, antipsychotics (dopamine antagonists); can blunt or amplify effects.[web:145][web:148]

**MAJOR CAUTIONS**

- **Adolescents:**  
  - Hormonal axis is still developing; use only for clear indications under practitioner supervision.

- **Psychiatric Medications:**  
  - Antipsychotics (dopamine antagonists) may be less effective; Vitex’s dopaminergic action may counteract them.[web:145][web:148]

- **Inconsistent Responses:**  
  - Some individuals experience worsened PMS or cycle disruption; monitor and discontinue if symptoms worsen.

**Drug Interactions:**  

- Dopaminergic medications (agonists and antagonists).[web:145][web:148]  
- Possible interactions with hormonal contraceptives (theoretical): data mixed; most clinicians allow concurrent use but monitor cycles and symptoms.

Primary Species Rosa canina L. / Rosa rubiginosa L. / Rosa spp.  
Rosehip, Rose Hip, Wild Rose Fruit  
Fruit of various rose species, especially dog rose and related wild roses across Europe, Western Asia, and other temperate regions. Hips are collected after ripening, often after first frost or once fully red-orange, then split and dried carefully.

Botanical Family  
Rosaceae rose family

Part Used  
Fruit (hip); sometimes deseeded shell or whole processed hip depending on preparation.

Harvesting Processing  
Fully ripe hips are harvested when bright red to orange-red, then cleaned and dried. Internal hairs are typically removed for comfort and safety in powder or tea preparations. Low-temperature drying helps preserve vitamin-rich integrity.

Quality Indicators  
Bright red-orange color, tart-fruity aroma, pleasantly sour taste, low moisture, minimal browning, no mold, and properly cleaned inner hairs.

Storage  
Airtight container in cool, dark, dry conditions; best quality often within 12–18 months.

Historical Context  
Used in European folk medicine and food traditions as a nutrient-dense tonic, especially valued for winter resilience, convalescence, and tart antioxidant support. Frequently made into teas, syrups, conserves, and powders.

CONTRAINDICATIONS INTERACTIONS

Safety Profile  
Generally very safe and food-like when properly prepared.

Major Cautions  
- Kidney stone susceptibility: Very high intake of concentrated vitamin-C-rich products may warrant caution in susceptible individuals.  
- Internal seed hairs: Improperly processed hips can irritate the throat or GI tract.  
- Blood-thinner or medication context: Usually low concern in food-like use, but concentrated extracts should still be reviewed clinically where necessary.  
- Significant acidity / sensitive stomach: Tart preparations may aggravate some people if taken too strongly on an empty stomach.

Herb-to-Herb Pairings  
- Synergistic: Hibiscus, elderberry, nettle, rose petals, hawthorn, cinnamon.  
- Caution: Very sour blends may aggravate highly acid-sensitive individuals.

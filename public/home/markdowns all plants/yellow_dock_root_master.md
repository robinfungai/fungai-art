# YELLOW DOCK (Rumex crispus) – ROOT

## Master Monograph: Iron‑Supportive Bitter & Mild Stimulant Laxative

**Primary Species:** Rumex crispus L. (Yellow dock, curly dock).[web:244][web:239]  
**Family:** Polygonaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Root**, dried and decocted or extractd.[web:244]

---

## FUNCTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**Energetics:**  
- Temperature: Cooling to neutral.  
- Taste: Bitter, astringent.  
- Actions: Digestive and hepatic bitter, mild stimulant laxative (anthraquinones), mineral/iron‑supportive.[web:244][web:239]

**Primary Targets:**  
- **Digestion & liver:** Sluggish digestion, mild liver congestion.  
- **Bowel:** Mild constipation supportive, especially in iron formulas.  
- **Blood / iron:** Often included in low‑dose iron‑support formulas with nettle and vitamin C.

**Core Principle:**  
“Yellow dock is a **mineral‑rich bitter with mild anthraquinone laxative action**—useful in short phases for constipation and iron support, but not as a chronic tonic due to oxalate and stimulant‑laxative concerns.”[web:244][web:239]

---

## PHARMACOLOGY

**Key Constituents:**

- **Anthraquinones:** Emodin, chrysophanol, others (stimulant laxative).  
- **Oxalates:** Significant content.  
- Tannins, iron and other minerals.

**Mechanisms:**

- **Laxative:**  
  - Anthraquinones increase colonic motility and secretion; excessive use → cramping/diarrhea, similar to other stimulant laxatives.[web:244][web:239]

- **Oxalate Issues:**  
  - Oxalate can bind calcium and contribute to kidney stones or kidney damage at high intake, especially in susceptible individuals; animal poisoning reported with high Rumex intake.[web:239]

---

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Typical Adult Internal:**

- Decoction: 1–2 g root in 250 ml water, gently simmered 10–15 min, 1–3× daily short term.  
- Extract 1:5: 1–4 ml, 1–3× daily.

**Use Pattern:**

- Better in **time‑limited phases** with clear goals (constipation reset, iron support) and then reassess; not daily long‑term tonic.[web:239]

---

## SAFETY & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Cautions (from monographs & reviews):**[web:244][web:239]

- **Pregnancy:** Avoid due to anthraquinone laxatives.  
- **Kidney Stones / Kidney Disease:** Oxalate content can worsen stones or renal issues.  
- **Existing bowel inflammation (IBD, hemorrhoids, fissures):** Stimulant laxative may aggravate.  
- **Electrolytes:** Excess use can increase potassium loss; additive with thiazide diuretics and other stimulant laxatives.[web:244][web:239]

**Interactions:**  

- Diuretics (especially thiazides) — potassium loss synergy.[web:244]  
- Other stimulant laxatives — increased risk of cramping, diarrhea, dependency.[web:244][web:239]

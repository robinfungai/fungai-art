## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:** Leonurus cardiaca L. — European native; naturalized across temperate regions globally. Named "Motherwort" (mother's herb) for traditional postpartum + menstrual support. Aerial parts (especially flowering tops) contain highest alkaloid concentration.
**Botanical Family:** Lamiaceae (mint family)
**Part Used:** Leonurus cardiaca herba — Aerial parts, leaves, flowering tops; fresh or dried; used as tincture, tea, powder.
**Harvesting & Processing:** Flowering tops harvested at peak bloom (summer, June-August); dried at low temperature (<40°C) to preserve leonurine alkaloids; stored in airtight containers away from light.
**Quality Indicators:** Whole aerial parts with visible flowers; distinctive aromatic smell (minty-herbal); purplish-pink flowers; slightly hairy stems. Taste: Intensely bitter + slightly acrid (leonurine signature).
**Storage:** Airtight glass, cool dark location; maintains potency 18-24 months (alkaloids relatively stable).

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**
**Primary Bioactives:**
- **Leonurine (Primary Alkaloid, ~0.05-0.3% of dried herb):** Cardiotonic alkaloid; positive inotropic (strengthens heart contraction), vasodilator, anti-arrhythmic. Also mild uterine stimulant (emmenagogue). Primary compound responsible for heart + reproductive effects.
**Supporting Compounds:**
- **Iridoid Glycosides (0.2-0.5%):** Leonuride, galiridoside (cardioprotective, anti-inflammatory, antispasmodic); supports heart function + emotional processing.
- **Alkaloids (Trace):** Stachydrine, betonicine (cardiovascular + neurotransmitter support).
- **Flavonoids (0.5-1.5%):** Rutin, hyperoside, quercetin, kaempferol (antioxidant, vascular support, anti-inflammatory, blood-brain barrier penetration).
- **Tannins (5-9%):** Astringent, antimicrobial, wound healing; contribute to earthiness + grounding effect.
- **Volatile Oils (0.1-0.3%):** Linalool (calming), α-pinene, other monoterpenes (aromatic, mild sedative properties).
**Mechanisms:**
- **Positive Inotropic Action (Leonurine):** Increases force of cardiac muscle contraction; strengthens heart's pumping capacity.
- **Vasodilation:** Widens blood vessels; improves circulation, particularly to heart muscle itself (coronary circulation).
- **Negative Chronotropic Action (Heart Rate Lowering):** Slows excessive heart rate (tachycardia), particularly anxiety-related; regulates rhythm (anti-arrhythmic).
- **Uterine Stimulation (Leonurine—Mild Emmenagogue):** Stimulates uterine contractions; promotes menstrual flow; tonifies uterine muscle over time.
- **Antispasmodic (Iridoid Glycosides):** Smooth muscle relaxation (uterine cramping, digestive tension).
- **Anti-Inflammatory & Antioxidant:** Reduces pro-inflammatory cytokines, protects tissue from oxidative damage.
- **Nervous System Modulation:** Mild GABAergic + serotonergic effects; nervous system calming without sedation.

Clinical significance: Particularly valuable for anxiety-related palpitations (heart racing from nervousness, panic). Does NOT "sedate" the heart (unlike beta-blockers); rather, optimizes function.
Traditional teaching: "Mother's heart must be strong"—strong enough to feel deeply (vulnerability) while remaining stable under pressure (resilience).

Distinction: Not a pharmaceutical cardiac drug; rather, herbal tonification. Works alongside meditation, breathwork, nervous system regulation—supports but doesn't replace these.

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:** MEDIUM CAUTION — Uterine stimulation; cycle-aware timing important. Generally safe long-term with appropriate use.

**Contraindications:**
**Pregnancy (Particularly Early Trimester):** Uterine-stimulating potential (leonurine mild emmenagogue). AVOID in first trimester (risk of uterine stimulation → potential miscarriage). Second/third trimester: Generally considered safe for postpartum preparation, but consult provider. AVOID high doses.
**Heavy Menstrual Bleeding (Menorrhagia):** Uterine stimulation may worsen flow. Use with caution or AVOID if tendency toward excessive bleeding.
**Breastfeeding:** Generally considered acceptable (traditional postpartum herb); limited infant safety data. Monitor infant for sedation (linalool may pass into milk). Continue if no adverse effects.
**Thyroid Conditions (Particularly Hyperthyroidism):** Minor concern; Motherwort may have mild goitrogenic activity (unconfirmed). Caution advised if thyroid disorders present.
**Cardiac Arrhythmias or Severe Cardiac Instability:** Use only under medical supervision. Leonurine's cardiac effects may interact with cardiac conditions (particularly arrhythmias). Consult cardiologist.

**Herb-to-Herb Synergy (Safe, Beneficial):**
**Hawthorn:** Comprehensive cardiovascular tonification; heart-supporting duo (Motherwort emotion + courage; Hawthorn cardiovascular grounding).
**Rose & Hawthorn:** Heart opening + strength (emotional + cardiovascular).
**Vitex & Raspberry Leaf:** Women's menstrual cycle support trinity; uterine tonification + cycle regulation.
**Lemon Balm, Skullcap, Lavender:** Nervous system support; enhances anxiety relief.
**Mugwort:** Menstrual regulation, dream work (synergistic emmenagogue effects; may potentiate).

**Herb-to-Herb Caution:**
**Other Uterine-Stimulating Herbs (Blue Cohosh, Pennyroyal, Rue, at high doses):** Coordinate carefully by menstrual cycle phase; combined strong stimulation may be excessive. Use lower individual doses if combining.
**Strong Cardiac Herbs (Digitalis, Convallaria—pharmaceutical cardiac plants):** Should NOT combine without medical coordination (unpredictable cardiac effects).

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**
**With Cardiac Medications (Beta-Blockers, Calcium Channel Blockers, Digoxin, Antiarrhythmics):** Monitor for additive cardiotonic effects. Leonurine's heart-slowing + strengthening may interact with medication action. Dose adjustment potentially needed; consult cardiologist/prescriber. Generally not contraindicated, but requires awareness + monitoring.
**With Hormone Therapies (Oral Contraceptives, HRT):** Potential interaction with uterine stimulation (theoretically could reduce contraceptive efficacy, though not well-documented). Monitor cycle regularity if combining. Generally considered safe, but awareness recommended.
**With MAOI Antidepressants:** Theoretical monoamine interaction (Motherwort may have weak MAOI activity—unconfirmed). Unlikely to be clinically significant; consult prescriber.
**Generally Safe:** With SSRIs, antianxiety medications, blood pressure medications (except cardiac meds noted above), other non-cardiac medications.

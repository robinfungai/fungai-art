# KAVA KAVA (Piper methysticum)

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Piper methysticum G. Forst. — Shrub native to Pacific Islands; dried peeled **rhizome** used to prepare ceremonial kava beverages.[web:193][web:277][web:285]  
**Botanical Family:** Piperaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Rhizome (and sometimes root)**, dried; aerial parts not used traditionally and may be more toxic.[web:193][web:277]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Roots/rhizomes harvested after several years, peeled and dried; ground into powder for cold‑water maceration. Modern extracts use ethanol/acetone (linked to some toxicity concerns).[web:193][web:277][web:276]  
**Quality Indicators:** Noble kava cultivars, peeled root‑only material, free from mold; kavalactone chemotype known.[web:193][web:285]  
**Storage:** Dried root and extracts airtight, cool/dry.  

---

## FUNCTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**Energetics:**  
- Temperature: Cooling to neutral.  
- Taste: Peppery, numbing, slightly bitter.  
- Actions: **Anxiolytic, sedative, muscle relaxant**, mild analgesic; historically ceremonial and social beverage.[web:193][web:285]

**Primary Target Systems:**  
- **CNS:** Anxiety, tension, insomnia; muscle tension and restlessness.[web:193]  
- **Musculoskeletal:** Muscle tightness, some forms of pain.

**Core Principle:**  
“Kava is a **GABAergic, kavalactone‑rich anxiolytic** with decent short‑term evidence for anxiety reduction but a **real, though rare, risk of serious hepatotoxicity**, especially with non‑traditional extracts or susceptible individuals.”[web:193][web:276][web:277][web:285]

---

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Kavalactones:** Kavain, dihydrokavain, methysticin, dihydromethysticin, yangonin, desmethoxyyangonin — responsible for CNS effects.[web:193][web:285]  
- Minor alkaloids, **flavokavains** (A, B, C) — suspected contributors to liver toxicity.[web:276][web:285]

**Mechanisms:**

- Modulation of **GABA‑A** receptors (non‑benzodiazepine; distinct binding sites) → anxiolysis and sedation.[web:193][web:285]  
- Interaction with voltage‑gated Na+ and Ca2+ channels; mild MAO‑B inhibition; effects on dopamine and serotonin reuptake; CB1 modulation.[web:193][web:285]  
- Net effect: decreased neuronal excitability, muscle relaxation, subjective calm.

**Hepatotoxicity:**

- WHO and national regulators analyzed **dozens of liver injury cases**, including acute liver failure and transplant.[web:276][web:193]  
- Risk factors proposed: acetonic/ethanolic extracts, use of aerial parts/peels, high doses, alcohol use, and genetic susceptibility; exact mechanism unresolved (reactive metabolites, glutathione depletion, flavokavains).[web:276][web:285]  
- Population data suggest incidence is **low but not negligible**; cannot be dismissed.[web:276][web:193]

---

## EVIDENCE FOR ANXIETY

- Multiple RCTs with standardized kava extracts (often 60–120 mg kavalactones/day) show **significant reductions in generalized anxiety scores vs placebo**, with effect sizes comparable to low‑dose benzodiazepines in some trials.[web:193][web:285]  
- Benefits often emerge over 1–2 weeks and continue through 4–8‑week trials.[web:193]

---

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Traditional Aqueous Kava:**

- Cold‑water maceration of powdered peeled root; doses vary widely but often equivalent to **60–250 mg kavalactones per session** in ceremonial settings.

**Standardized Extracts (Modern):**

- Common clinical dosing: **60–120 mg total kavalactones/day**, sometimes up to 240 mg/day short‑term.[web:193][web:277]  
- Many guidelines advise **≤120 mg/day** for self‑care.

**Duration:**

- Most authorities recommend limiting continuous use to **no more than 3 months**, with breaks and liver monitoring in at‑risk individuals.[web:193][web:276]

---

## ADVERSE EFFECTS

- Common: Mild GI upset, dizziness, headache, drowsiness, kava dermopathy (with very high traditional use).[web:193][web:276]  
- Serious: **Liver injury** (cholestatic or mixed hepatitis), rarely fulminant; typically within 2–12 weeks of use.[web:276][web:193]  

---

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Absolute / Strong Contraindications:**

- Pre‑existing **liver disease** or abnormal LFTs.[web:276][web:193][web:277]  
- Concurrent **hepatotoxic drugs** (e.g., high‑dose acetaminophen, isoniazid, certain TKIs).[web:276]  
- **Alcohol abuse** or heavy regular alcohol consumption.[web:276][web:285]  
- **Pregnancy and lactation.**  
- History of **allergic reaction** to kava.

**Major Interactions:**

- **CNS depressants** (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, sedative antihistamines, alcohol, opioids): additive sedation and psychomotor impairment.[web:193][web:285]  
- Potential interactions with **dopaminergic, serotonergic, and GABAergic drugs** (less clearly defined).

---

## RESEARCH GRADE & SUMMARY

| Domain                   | Grade | Notes |
|--------------------------|-------|-------|
| Anxiolytic efficacy      | B+    | Multiple RCTs with consistent short‑term benefit[web:193][web:285] |
| Hepatotoxic risk         | B+    | Case series + mechanistic work; low incidence but serious[web:276][web:193] |
| Long‑term safety         | C     | Limited beyond 3 months |

---

## AI APP INTEGRATION

- Treat kava as **restricted, short‑term anxiolytic**.  
- Mandatory screening: liver disease, alcohol intake, hepatotoxic meds, pregnancy, concurrent sedatives.  
- Offer safer nervines (passionflower, skullcap, linden, lemon balm, oatstraw, rose) as default; reserve kava for advanced/practitioner or explicit informed‑risk context.

# KRATOM (Mitragyna speciosa) – RISK MONOGRAPH

## Master Monograph: Opioid‑like Tree With Dependence & Toxicity Risks

**Primary Species:** Mitragyna speciosa (Korth.) Havil. — Tropical tree native to Southeast Asia; leaves used traditionally for stimulation at low doses and opioid‑like analgesia at higher doses.[web:199][web:280][web:278]  
**Family:** Rubiaceae.  
**Part Used:** **Leaf**, fresh or dried (teas, powders, extracts).[web:199][web:280]

---

## FUNCTIONAL / RISK FRAMEWORK

**Energetics:** Neutral to slightly warming.  
**Actions:** Dose‑dependent **stimulant and opioid‑like analgesic**, antidiarrheal, cough suppressant.[web:199][web:278][web:280]

**Core Principle:**  
“Kratom is a **poly‑alkaloid, μ‑opioid–active plant** that behaves closer to a complex opioid drug than a gentle herb, with significant risks of **dependence, withdrawal, drug interactions, and toxicity**.”[web:199][web:278][web:280]

---

## PHARMACOLOGY

**Key Alkaloids:**

- **Mitragynine** (major) and **7‑hydroxymitragynine** (more potent μ‑opioid agonist), plus paynantheine, speciociliatine, speciogynine, etc.[web:199][web:280]

**Mechanisms:**

- **μ‑Opioid receptor partial agonism** → analgesia, euphoria, respiratory depression at high doses.[web:199][web:280]  
- Activity at adrenergic (α2), serotonergic, and other receptors; complex CNS profile (stimulation at low dose, sedation at high).[web:199][web:278]

**Metabolism & Interactions:**

- Kratom alkaloids inhibit **CYP3A4 and CYP2D6**, with IC50s in ranges suggesting clinically meaningful interactions.[web:194][web:199][web:280]  
- This can raise levels of co‑administered drugs (opioids, benzodiazepines, antidepressants, antipsychotics, etc.), increasing toxicity risk.

---

## CLINICAL & EPIDEMIOLOGICAL DATA

- Widely self‑used for **chronic pain, opioid withdrawal, mood disorders**, but **evidence is largely anecdotal**, with very limited controlled trials.[web:199][web:280]  
- Surveillance data: kratom involved in **thousands of poison‑center calls**; symptoms include agitation, tachycardia, seizures, coma; deaths usually involve other substances but some cases list kratom as primary contributor.[web:199][web:280]

**Dependence & Withdrawal:**

- Regular high‑dose use produces tolerance and **opioid‑like withdrawal**: muscle aches, insomnia, irritability, diarrhea, anxiety, rhinorrhea.[web:199][web:280]

---

## SAFETY & REGULATORY STATUS

- FDA and many toxicology reviews warn about **serious risks** and lack of established safe dosing.[web:199][web:280]  
- Several countries (e.g., Thailand previously, some EU states) have restricted or banned kratom; regulatory status is evolving.[web:199][web:278]

---

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & APP STANCE

**Absolute Contraindications:**

- History of **substance use disorder** (especially opioids, sedatives, stimulants).  
- Concurrent use of **opioids, benzodiazepines, alcohol, barbiturates, gabapentinoids, or other CNS depressants**.  
- Significant liver or kidney disease.  
- Serious psychiatric conditions (psychosis, bipolar) due to reports of psychotic reactions.[web:199][web:280]  
- Pregnancy & lactation.

**App Policy Recommendation:**

- Treat kratom as **“do‑not‑recommend / excluded herb.”**  
- If user asks, clearly explain:  
  - μ‑opioid receptor activity and dependence/withdrawal.  
  - CYP3A4/2D6 inhibition and overdose synergy with medications.  
  - Uncertain long‑term safety and regulatory warnings.[web:199][web:194][web:280]

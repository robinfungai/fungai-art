# CHILCUAGUE / CHILCUAGUE ROOT  
*Heliopsis longipes*

---

## OVERVIEW

**Common Names:** Chilcuague, Aztec root, Golden root (Mexico)  
**Plant Part:** Root (fresh or dried)  
**Primary Actions:** Strong oral analgesic/anesthetic; antifungal and antibacterial; pungent digestive stimulant; sialagogue.[web:220][web:222][web:224][web:226][web:227]

Chilcuague is a traditional Mexican ethnobotanical used for **toothache, oral infections, sore throat, and digestive/respiratory issues**, famous for its intense tingling and numbing sensations in the mouth.

---

## KEY CONSTITUENTS

- **Alkamide affinin** (isobutylamide) — principal pungent and anesthetic component.[web:218][web:220][web:224]  
- Other alkamides and essential oil constituents.  

These alkamides produce strong tingling, salivation, transient numbness, and local analgesia, alongside antimicrobial and antifungal effects.[web:220][web:224][web:226]

---

## TRADITIONAL & MODERN USES

### 1. Oral Analgesic & Antimicrobial

- Chewed or applied to **toothache, inflamed gums, mouth ulcers, sore throats**, and oral infections.[web:220][web:222][web:224][web:226]  
- Acts as a **local anesthetic + antiseptic**, increasing salivation and mucosal circulation.

### 2. Digestive & Appetite Stimulant

- Pungent root stimulates salivation and gastric secretions; used for **indigestion, low appetite, and sluggish digestion**.[web:220][web:224]  
- Traditionally taken before meals or as a extract/chewed piece.

### 3. Antifungal, Antibacterial, Insecticidal

- In vitro studies and traditional use show **antifungal and bactericidal** properties (e.g., on Candida and oral pathogens).[web:218][web:220][web:224]  
- Used externally for fungal skin issues and as a natural insect repellent in some communities.

### 4. Respiratory Support

- Sometimes used for **sore throat, early cold/flu, and respiratory congestion**, likely via increased salivation, antimicrobial action, and reflex effects.[web:220][web:226]

---

## ENERGETICS & TISSUE STATE

- **Temperature:** Hot, stimulating  
- **Moisture:** Initially moistening (salivation), then slightly drying  
- **Taste:** Strongly pungent, tingling, numbing  
- **Tissue States:** Cold, stagnant, poorly perfused oral/digestive mucosa; low secretions; functional “cold/damp” patterns.

Best for **cold, sluggish, congested** oral and digestive patterns, not for red, hot, inflamed tissues already aggravated by pungents.

---

## PREPARATIONS & DOSING

### Chewed Root

- Tiny piece of dried or fresh root chewed until tingling and salivation start; then either spit out or swallow.  
- Used for toothache, gum pain, sore throat.

### Extract

- Root extractd 1:3–1:5 in high‑proof alcohol (at least 50–60% to capture alkamides).[web:222][web:224]  
- Dose: **Low‑dose herb** — typically 1–10 drops at a time, up to several times daily, due to potency.

**Topical use:**

- 1–5 drops in a little water as a **swish/gargle** for oral infections and sore throat, then spit.  
- Avoid high doses swallowed repeatedly due to limited systemic data.

---

## SAFETY, CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**General:**  
- Traditional use suggests good safety at low, topical and small internal doses; modern toxicology is limited.[web:220][web:224][web:226]

**Cautions:**

- Very **pungent and intense**: high doses can irritate or burn mucosa; always start low.  
- Avoid on **severely inflamed, ulcerated, or hypersensitive mucosa** where strong pungent may aggravate.  
- Pregnancy & lactation: insufficient data; avoid or keep to very small topical doses.  
- Children: use with caution and only tiny topical doses if at all.

**Interactions:**

- Theoretically may increase absorption of other drugs/supplements taken simultaneously via increased blood flow and secretions, but data are minimal.

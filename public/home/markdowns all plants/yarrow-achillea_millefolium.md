## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Achillea millefolium L. (Yarrow, Milfoil) — Perennial herb native to temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere; common in meadows, roadsides, and grasslands. Aerial parts (leaves and flowering tops) harvested at early to full bloom.[web:30][web:36]

**Botanical Family:**  
Asteraceae (daisy family).[web:30]

**Part Used:**  
Millefolii herba — Dried aerial parts: feathery leaves and white to pinkish flower clusters.[web:30]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Cut upper flowering tops on dry days, avoiding older, browned flowers; dry in thin layers in shade with airflow; store whole and crumble as needed.[web:30]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Aromatic, with characteristic pungent, slightly bitter scent; green-grey leaves and intact flower clusters; minimal browning, no mold.[web:30]

**Storage:**  
Airtight, dark, dry; best potency 1–2 years.[web:30]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Tea/Infusion:**  
- 1–2 tsp dried herb per 250 ml boiling water; steep 10–15 minutes.  
- For acute fever/diaphoretic use: drink hot, 3–5 cups/day for short periods.  
- For digestion/circulation: 1–3 cups/day.[web:30]

**Extract:**  
- 1:5 in 40–60% alcohol: 2–4 ml 2–3× daily.  
- For acute fever: 1–2 ml every 1–3 hours (short term).

**Topical:**  
- Fresh poultice: crushed fresh leaves applied to cuts/wounds.  
- Strong infusion as wash or sitz bath for wounds, hemorrhoids, or vaginal tissue toning.

**Duration:**  
- Acute fever: short-term (days).  
- Digestive/circulatory: weeks to months, with breaks.  

**Best Preparations & Protocols:**  

**Protocol 1: Early-Stage Cold/Flu (Diaphoretic)**

**Protocol 2: Heavy / Spasmodic Menstruation**

**Protocol 3: Wound / Nosebleed**

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:**  
GRADE B — Generally safe, but notable cautions for pregnancy and Asteraceae allergy.[web:30][web:36]

**Contraindications / Cautions:**  
- **Pregnancy:** Avoid; uterine-stimulating and emmenagogue potentials may increase miscarriage risk.[web:30][web:36]  
- **Asteraceae Allergy:** Avoid in individuals with ragweed/chamomile/yarrow family allergy; may trigger dermatitis or more severe reactions.[web:30][web:36]  
- **Photosensitivity:** Rarely, may contribute to photosensitization in some sensitive individuals.[web:36]  

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**  
- None well documented; theoretically caution with anticoagulants due to hemostatic and vascular effects, though evidence is limited.[web:30]

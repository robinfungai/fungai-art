# PETASITES HYBRIDUS (BUTTERBUR)  
*Petasites hybridus*

---

## OVERVIEW

**Common Names:** Butterbur, Sweet Coltsfoot, Pestwurz (German)  
**Plant Part:** Root and rhizome (standardized PA‑free extract); occasionally leaf  
**Primary Actions:** Anti‑migraine; antispasmodic; anti‑allergic (rhinitis); bronchial relaxant.[web:213][web:215][web:219][web:221]

Butterbur is a European marsh/riverbank plant whose PA‑free root extracts have strong evidence for **migraine prevention** and **allergic rhinitis**, acting via leukotriene inhibition, TRPA1 channels, and smooth‑muscle/vascular modulation.[web:213][web:214][web:217][web:223]

---

## KEY CONSTITUENTS

- Sesquiterpene esters: **Petasin, isopetasin** (key active anti‑migraine and spasmolytic constituents)[web:213][web:219][web:225]  
- Pyrrolizidine alkaloids (PAs) — **hepatotoxic**; must be removed in internal products[web:215][web:216][web:221]  
- Flavonoids, tannins.

Modern internal use relies on **PA‑free standardized extracts** to avoid liver toxicity.[web:215][web:216][web:221]

---

## CLINICAL USES

### 1. Migraine Prophylaxis

- Multiple RCTs show **PA‑free butterbur root extract** reduces migraine attack frequency and days, with doses around 2×25–75 mg standardized extract/day.[web:214][web:217][web:219]  
- Mechanism: TRPA1 desensitization, leukotriene and prostaglandin inhibition, vascular smooth‑muscle relaxation.[web:213][web:219][web:225]

Used as a **preventive**, not acute painkiller, over weeks–months.

### 2. Allergic Rhinitis / Hay Fever

- Clinical trials show butterbur extract is **as effective as cetirizine** in seasonal allergic rhinitis, with fewer sedating effects, when PA‑free standardized products are used.[web:214][web:221][web:223]  
- Benefits: reduced sneezing, nasal congestion, itchy/watery eyes; some bronchial relief.

Fits well in **pollen allergy / hay fever** and **cat dander allergy** protocols as a focused anti‑allergic component.

### 3. Bronchial Spasm & Asthma

- Traditional use and modern data support **bronchodilatory and antispasmodic effects** in asthma and COPD; likely via leukotriene and calcium‑channel modulation.[web:215][web:216]  
- Typically adjunct to standard medical therapy.

---

## ENERGETICS & TISSUE STATE

- **Temperature:** Slightly cooling  
- **Moisture:** Slightly drying  
- **Taste:** Bitter‑aromatic  
- **Tissue States:** Hot, inflamed, spasmodic mucosa and vasculature; allergic/hyper‑reactive tissues.

Best suited for **hot, congestive, spastic** patterns (migraine, allergic rhinitis, bronchial spasm).

---

## PREPARATIONS & DOSING

**Important:** Internal use should be **PA‑free standardized extract only**, not crude root, to avoid liver toxicity.[web:215][web:216][web:221]

### Standardized Extract

- Typical migraine dose: 50–75 mg PA‑free extract **2×/day**, with meals, for at least 3–4 months.[web:214][web:217][web:219]  
- Allergic rhinitis: similar doses (per product instructions) during allergy season.[web:221][web:223]

### Extract

For your system, treat butterbur as a **standardized extract ingredient** rather than a home‑made extract, or if extractd, use PA‑removed bulk material.

- Dose (if PA‑free): 0.5–1 ml, 2–3×/day in blends, for limited courses.

---

## SAFETY, CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Crude, PA‑containing butterbur is hepatotoxic** and **not recommended** internally.[web:215][web:216][web:221]

Use **only** certified PA‑free products.

**Cautions:**

- **Liver disease or hepatotoxic meds:** Avoid or use only with close monitoring; even PA‑free extracts warrant caution.[web:215][web:221]  
- **Pregnancy & Lactation:** Not enough safety data; generally **avoid** for internal use.[web:221]  
- **Children:** Some pediatric use in migraine/allergy studies, but only with PA‑free standardized products and medical supervision.[web:216][web:221]

**Interactions:**

- Theoretical additive effects with other hepatotoxic herbs/drugs.  
- May complement antihistamines or leukotriene antagonists, but data are limited.

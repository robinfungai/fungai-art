# HORSETAIL (Equisetum arvense)
## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** *Equisetum arvense* L. (Field Horsetail, Common Horsetail) — widely distributed across Europe, Asia, North America; prefers sandy, moist soils and field margins.[web:110][web:118]  
**Botanical Family:** Equisetaceae (ancient vascular plant lineage; “living fossil”).  
**Part Used:** *Equiseti herba* — **Sterile aerial shoots** (green, non‑spore‑bearing stems). Fertile brown shoots in early spring are not used medicinally.  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Sterile aerial stems cut in early–mid summer when fully green and robust; dried promptly at low temperature in shade to preserve flavonoids and silica; stored whole and cut/sifted as needed.[web:110][web:116]  
**Quality Indicators:** Clean, bright green to yellowish‑green stems; rough, silica‑rich texture; no mold or darkened/brown, musty material. Little to no root or rhizome contamination.  
**Storage:** Airtight containers, cool dark place; maintains good quality 12–24 months.  
**Historical Context:** Used since antiquity as a **diuretic, wound‑healing agent, and bone/connective‑tissue support** (“scouring rush” used to polish metal/wood). Recognised in European pharmacopoeias and EMA as a traditional herbal medicinal product to **increase urine output for flushing the urinary tract** in minor urinary conditions.[web:110][web:116][web:118]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds**

**Primary Bioactives (Aerial Parts):**

- **Silica / Silicic Acid (5–8% of dry herb):** Present as soluble and insoluble forms; contributes to connective tissue and bone mineralisation, collagen cross‑linking, and structural integrity.[web:112][web:113][web:117][web:121]  
- **Flavonoids:** Quercetin, Kaempferol, Apigenin derivatives (e.g., isoquercitrin, luteolin glycosides) — antioxidant, mild diuretic, vasoprotective.[web:112][web:118][web:122]  
- **Phenolic Acids:** Caffeic, Ferulic, p‑Coumaric acids — antioxidant, mild anti‑inflammatory.[web:112]  
- **Saponins & Triterpenoids:** Contribute to diuretic, vascular, and smooth‑muscle effects.[web:112][web:118]  
- **Minerals:** Calcium, potassium, magnesium, manganese — supportive for bone and electrolyte balance.  

**Other Constituents:**

- **Alkaloids (e.g., Equisetin, trace nicotine):** Trace amounts; not primary drivers of clinical action.  
- **Thiaminase:** Enzyme that degrades thiamine (vitamin B1); significant in raw plant and in some toxic species (e.g., *E. palustre*), less concern when properly dried/heated and used in moderate therapeutic doses.[web:112][web:122]

## KEY MECHANISMS

### 1. Diuretic – Urinary Flushing (Silica + Flavonoids + Saponins)

In a randomized, double‑blind clinical trial, a dried horsetail extract (900 mg/day) increased urine volume comparably to hydrochlorothiazide, but without significant changes in electrolyte excretion.[web:114][web:118]

- **Effect:**  
  - Increased urine output, supporting “flushing” of the urinary tract in minor infections, gravel, or low‑grade irritation.[web:110][web:114][web:116]  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Diuretic effect observable within days; typical traditional courses 2–4 weeks.[web:110][web:116][web:124]  
- **Clinical significance:**  
  - Useful as an **adjuvant** in minor lower urinary tract complaints, renal gravel, or mild oedema in individuals with otherwise normal heart/kidney function.

### 2. Bone & Connective Tissue Mineralisation (Silica Focus)

Silica is essential for collagen matrix formation and bone mineral deposition; animal studies using *E. arvense* combined with nutrient formulations show enhanced bone density, cortical thickness, and improved biomarkers vs. controls.[web:117][web:121]

- **Effect:**  
  - Supports bone mineral density and microarchitecture in experimental models; traditional use for osteoporosis, fractures, and degenerative joint issues.[web:117][web:118][web:121]  
- **Timeline:**  
  - Cumulative; months for structural support; best as part of a broad bone health strategy (Ca, vitamin D, protein, movement).  
- **Clinical significance:**  
  - Considered a **supportive, not stand‑alone,** strategy for osteopenia/osteoporosis, connective tissue fragility, and slow tissue healing.

### 3. Tissue Toning & Boundary Support

Tannins, silica, and minerals contribute to **astringent, toning effects** on mucosa and connective tissue.

- **Effect:**  
  - Tighter, more resilient tissues (skin, gums, urinary lining, connective tissue), improved hair/nail robustness.[web:113][web:118]  
- **Clinical significance:**  
  - Aligns with traditional use in **wounds, varicose veins, hemorrhoids**, and as a cosmetic hair/nail tonic.

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

*(Based on EMA/HMPC monograph and clinical data.)*

### Tea / Infusion

- **Dried aerial herb:** ~2 g per 150 ml boiling water (approx. 1–2 tsp); steep 10–15 minutes; strain.[web:110][web:116][web:124]  
- **Dose:** 2–4× daily between meals; daily dose around **6 g** dried herb (traditional).  

### Extract

- **Typical strength:** 1:5 in 25–40% ethanol (or similar).[web:113]  
- **Dose (adult):** 2–4 ml, 2–3× daily, depending on concentrate strength.

### Standardised Dried Extract

- Clinical trial: **900 mg/day** of standardized dried extract (EADE), divided doses, showed diuresis comparable to hydrochlorothiazide.[web:114]  

### Duration

- **Urinary / diuretic use:** Usually **2–4 weeks** as an adjuvant; not for chronic indefinite heavy use, especially in those with borderline renal or cardiac function.[web:110][web:116]  
- **Bone/connective tissue support:** Longer courses (3–6+ months) at moderate doses within a comprehensive bone protocol; monitor as appropriate.

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

### Benefits

- **Diuretic / urinary flushing:** Traditional and clinical support for mild urinary complaints and renal gravel flushing.[web:110][web:114][web:118]  
- **Bone & connective tissue:** Silica‑mediated support of bone, joint, and connective tissue structure; potential adjunct in osteoporosis and degenerative joint disease.[web:117][web:121]  
- **Hair, skin, nails:** Traditional use as beauty tonic; silica and minerals support keratinised tissues.[web:113][web:118]  
- **Mild anti‑inflammatory / antioxidant:** Supports tissue healing and reduces oxidative stress.[web:112][web:118][web:122]

---

### Safety Profile

**Overall Safety:**  
- Widely used, generally well tolerated at traditional doses.[web:110][web:112][web:118][web:123]  

**Key Concerns:**

- **Thiaminase Content:**  
  - Raw horsetail and some species contain thiaminase, which can degrade vitamin B1; risk is mainly with **large, chronic ingestion** or in livestock.[web:112][web:122]  
  - Proper drying/heat and therapeutic dosing in humans have **not** been clearly linked to clinical thiamine deficiency, but **long‑term high‑dose use** should ensure adequate B1 intake.

- **Renal / Cardiac Disease:**  
  - As with other diuretic herbs, **avoid or use only under specialist supervision** in patients with serious kidney disease or heart failure; EMA emphasises use only in **otherwise healthy** adults for minor urinary complaints.[web:110][web:116][web:120]

- **Liver Disease:**  
  - Rare case report of liver injury in a patient taking high amounts of horsetail juice with chronic hepatitis B; causality uncertain but suggests caution in **pre‑existing liver disease** and avoidance of high doses.[web:115]

**Contraindications / Cautions:**

- **Severe kidney or heart disease, oedema from organ failure:** Do not use as self‑treatment diuretic.[web:110][web:116]  
- **Pregnancy & Breastfeeding:** Insufficient safety data; generally avoided in concentrated medicinal doses; occasional tea use is sometimes allowed under practitioner guidance.  
- **Children:** EMA monograph typically restricts to adults; safety in children not well established.[web:110][web:124]  
- **Thiamine Deficiency / Alcoholism / Malnutrition:** Use cautiously; ensure B‑complex support.

**Adverse Effects:**

- Minor: GI upset, diarrhea, mild abdominal discomfort reported at times.[web:115][web:123]  
- Rare: Allergic reactions; single case report of liver injury at very high intake with underlying hepatitis B.[web:115]

**Drug Interactions:**

- **Diuretics:** Potential additive effects; monitor electrolytes and blood pressure if combined.  
- **Lithium:** Theoretic interaction due to increased diuresis altering lithium clearance.  
- **Antihypertensives:** Increased diuresis may contribute to hypotension in some patients.

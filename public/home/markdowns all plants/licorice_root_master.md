## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Glycyrrhiza glabra L. (European licorice) and Glycyrrhiza uralensis Fisch. (Chinese licorice, Gan Cao) — Leguminous perennials native to Europe/Asia; roots harvested after several years’ growth.[web:33][web:46][web:49]

**Botanical Family:**  
Fabaceae.[web:49]

**Part Used:**  
Glycyrrhizae radix — Dried roots and stolons, cut and often sliced or powdered.[web:49]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Roots dug in autumn, cleaned, sliced, dried; sometimes roasted (Zhì Gān Cǎo) in TCM for modified energetics. Deglycyrrhizinated licorice (DGL) is processed to remove most glycyrrhizin for safer GI use.[web:46][web:49]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Yellowish-brown sweet roots with characteristic odor; high glycyrrhizin content (~2–15% depending on species/preparation).[web:46][web:49]

**Storage:**  
Dry, airtight, dark; stable for several years.

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**  

**Primary Bioactives:**  
- **Glycyrrhizin (glycyrrhizic acid, 2–15%):** Saponin glycoside; metabolized to glycyrrhetinic acid; mineralocorticoid-like effects via 11β-HSD2 inhibition.[web:33][web:46]  
- **Flavonoids:** Liquiritin, isoliquiritin, glabridin, etc. — anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, antispasmodic.[web:46]  
- **Polysaccharides:** Immunomodulatory and demulcent.

**Mechanisms:**  
- **Pseudoaldosteronism:** Glycyrrhetinic acid inhibits 11β-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2, allowing cortisol to stimulate mineralocorticoid receptors → sodium and water retention, potassium loss, hypertension, edema.[web:33][web:46][web:49]  
- **Anti-inflammatory:** Downregulates pro-inflammatory cytokines and enzymes (COX, LOX) and stabilizes cell membranes.[web:46]  
- **Hepatoprotective:** Glycyrrhizin/glycyrrhetinic acid used as drugs in Japan/China for chronic hepatitis; reduce liver inflammation and fibrosis in some contexts.[web:46]  
- **Demulcent & Anti-ulcer:** Coats and soothes mucosa, increases mucin production; DGL especially used for gastric and esophageal ulcers and reflux.[web:33][web:46]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. Mineralocorticoid-Mimetic (PRIMARY SAFETY ISSUE)**  
Chronic ingestion of significant glycyrrhizin doses causes pseudoaldosteronism: hypertension, hypokalemia, edema, and related sequelae.[web:33][web:46][web:49]  
Effect: Elevated BP, fluid retention, weakness, arrhythmia risk; can be serious.  
Threshold: In many adults, >3 g/day crude root or >100 mg/day glycyrrhizin for >6 weeks increases risk, though sensitive individuals may react at lower doses.[web:33]

**2. Anti-Inflammatory & Mucosal Protective**  
Reduces inflammatory mediators and enhances mucus; beneficial in gastritis, ulcers, and respiratory inflammation (cough, bronchitis).[web:46]  
Effect: Reduced pain and irritation, improved healing.

**3. Hepatoprotective & Antiviral**  
Glycyrrhizin-based preparations used for chronic hepatitis and liver protection; antiviral activity against certain viruses in vitro and in clinical use contexts.[web:46]

**4. Antitussive & Expectorant**  
Soothes cough, moistens respiratory mucosa, and thins mucus; frequently used in cough syrups and lung formulas.[web:46]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Crude Root (Non-DGL):**  
- Typical safe adult oral dose for short-term use: 1–3 g/day dried root, divided.[web:33][web:46][web:49]  
- Many sources recommend limiting continuous use of >3 g/day to ≤6 weeks to reduce pseudoaldosteronism risk.[web:33]

**Tea/Decoction:**  
- 1–2 tsp (~2–4 g) cut root per 250 ml water; simmer 10–20 minutes; 1–3× daily.  
- Often used as 5–15% of a multi-herb formula.

**Tincture (Whole Root):**  
- 1:5 (40–60% alcohol): 1–2 ml 2–3× daily (short-term), always factoring total daily glycyrrhizin load.

**Deglycyrrhizinated Licorice (DGL):**  
- Chewable tablets or powder: 380–760 mg 20 minutes before meals for GERD/ulcer support in many protocols. Glycyrrhizin mostly removed → minimal pseudoaldosteronism risk.[web:33][web:46]

**Duration:**  
- Whole-root (glycyrrhizin-containing): Preferably ≤4–6 weeks continuous unless monitored (BP, electrolytes).[web:33][web:49]  
- DGL: Often safe for longer-term mucosal support (under supervision for complex cases).

**Best Preparations & Protocols:**  

**Protocol 1: Acute Respiratory Inflammation / Cough**  
- 1–2 g root in tea or formula, 2–3× daily for ≤2–3 weeks; combine with thyme, mullein, marshmallow root.

**Protocol 2: Gastritis / Ulcer (DGL Preferred)**  
- DGL chewable 380 mg, 1–2 tablets 20 minutes before meals and at bedtime, typically 8–12 weeks; monitor symptoms and, if needed, coordinate care with GI specialist.[web:33][web:46]

**Protocol 3: Adrenal / Stress Support (Cautious)**  
- Small doses (e.g., 1–2 g/day) for individuals with low blood pressure and stress-related fatigue, limited to several weeks; monitor BP and potassium.

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:**  
GRADE B- — Highly valuable but with well-documented, dose-dependent cardiovascular and electrolyte risks.

**Contraindications / Strong Cautions (Whole Root / Glycyrrhizin-Containing):**  
- Hypertension (especially uncontrolled).[web:33][web:46][web:49]  
- Cardiovascular disease (heart failure, coronary disease, arrhythmias).[web:49]  
- Kidney disease (impaired ability to handle fluid/electrolyte shifts).[web:49]  
- Hypokalemia; users on loop/thiazide diuretics or corticosteroids.[web:33][web:49]  
- Pregnancy: Chronic or high-dose use discouraged due to BP and electrolyte concerns.[web:49]  

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**  
- **Antihypertensives:** May counteract BP-lowering effect.[web:49]  
- **Diuretics:** Additive hypokalemia and volume changes.[web:33][web:49]  
- **Corticosteroids:** Potentiates mineralocorticoid effects, increasing risk of edema and hypertension.[web:33][web:46][web:49]  
- **Cardiac Glycosides (digoxin):** Hypokalemia increases toxicity risk.[web:33]

## CLINICAL NOTES & PRACTITIONER GUIDANCE

- Always screen for BP history, cardiovascular and renal status before recommending sustained licorice usage.  
- For long-term mucosal support, strongly consider DGL to avoid mineralocorticoid issues.  
- Include potassium-rich foods or supplementation when using glycyrrhizin-containing whole root beyond brief periods, and monitor BP/electrolytes for at-risk individuals.[web:33][web:46]  
- Use lower doses and shorter durations in older adults and those with borderline BP.

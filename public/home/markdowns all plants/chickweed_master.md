# CHICKWEED (Stellaria media)

## Master Monograph: Complete Integration Across All Departments

**Primary Species:** Stellaria media (L.) Vill. (Chickweed, Common chickweed) — Delicate, low‑growing annual found worldwide, especially in moist, nutrient‑rich soils and gardens; edible “weed” and cooling vulnerary.[web:99][web:112][web:117]  
**Botanical Family:** Caryophyllaceae (pink/carnation family).[web:112][web:117]  
**Part Used:** **Aerial parts** (fresh or dried herb) — stems, leaves, flowers; often used fresh for succus or poultice.[web:99][web:102][web:117]  
**Harvesting & Processing:** Harvest tender aerial parts before flowering peak; use fresh or dry quickly in shade with airflow (thin layers). Succus (fresh juice) made from just‑picked plants.[web:99][web:102]  
**Quality Indicators:** Lush, bright green, succulent stems and leaves; mild, green, slightly salty taste; no yellowing or sliminess. Dried herb retains green color and mild aroma (not brown/musty).[web:99][web:112]  
**Storage:** Dried herb in airtight glass, cool and dark; best within 1 year. Fresh succus frozen in small portions if needed.[web:99]  
**Historical Context:** Traditional European and North American herb for **itching, hot skin diseases, ulcers, mild rheumatism, and “spring clearing” of heat and damp**; eaten as salad/“wild green.”[web:99][web:100][web:109]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Key Constituents:**

- **Saponins:** Contribute to expectorant, anti‑inflammatory, and mild detergent/penetrant effects; high intake may cause GI upset.[web:99][web:115]  
- **Flavonoids & Phenolic Compounds:** Luteolin, apigenin, rutin, and others with antioxidant, anti‑inflammatory actions.[web:115][web:122]  
- **Mucilage & Polysaccharides:** Demulcent, soothing to mucous membranes and skin; anti‑ulcer effects.[web:115][web:119]  
- **Minerals & Vitamins:** Rich in minerals (iron, calcium, magnesium) and vitamin C, contributing to tonic effects when eaten as food.[web:112][web:115]

**Mechanisms (In vitro / In vivo):**

- **Anti‑inflammatory / Anti‑pruritic:** Extracts inhibit inflammatory mediators and reduce edema in various models; topical use diminishes itch and irritation.[web:115][web:122]  
- **Anti‑ulcer & Gastroprotective:** Chickweed extracts show protection against experimentally induced gastric ulcers and support mucosal healing, likely via mucilage, antioxidants, and saponin actions.[web:115][web:119]  
- **Antioxidant:** Significant free‑radical scavenging and antioxidant activity in multiple assays.[web:115][web:122]  
- **Mild Diuretic & Metabolic Effects:** Traditional diuretic; network pharmacology work suggests potential roles in cardiometabolic support via anti‑inflammatory/antioxidant pathways, but this remains preliminary.[web:103][web:122]

**1. Skin Soother & Anti‑itch Vulnerary (Topical)**

Effect: Reduced itching, redness, heat, and irritation in eczema, psoriasis, insect bites, hives, minor burns, and rashes.  
Timeline: Often noticeable within days; used repeatedly for persistent conditions.  
Clinical significance: Core ingredient in **eczema, psoriasis, and hot‑itch salves** and washes.

**2. Demulcent Anti‑ulcer & GI Cooler (Internal)**  
Mucilage and anti‑ulcer activity protect gastric mucosa and reduce acid‑induced damage.[web:115][web:119]  
Effect: Soothing to gastritis, peptic ulcer, and hot, irritated upper GI; mild support for reflux when combined with diet and other demulcents.  
Timeline: Several days to weeks for mucosal calm; best used with triggers reduced.  
Clinical significance: Gentle, food‑like GI supporter for “heat and irritation” patterns.

**3. Lymph & Damp‑Heat Modulator**  
Traditionally used to “cool and clear” low‑grade inflammatory states with lymphatic congestion and damp.[web:99][web:106][web:103]  
Effect: Softens swollen lymph nodes, reduces boggy edema, and helps clear metabolic “sludge” when used as spring tonic food/herb.  
Timeline: Weeks of regular use.  
Clinical significance: Mild adjunct in skin‑lymph connection patterns and “spring clearing” formulas.

**4. Mild Diuretic / Urinary Soother**  
Mildly increases urine output and soothes urinary tract when inflamed.[web:99][web:103]  
Effect: Gentle “water metabolism” support in damp‑heat urinary patterns (burning/irritation alongside other herbs).  
Clinical significance: Adjunct, not stand‑alone therapy, for mild UT irritation.

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Food Use:**  
- Fresh leaves in salads, pestos, smoothies; handful amounts daily in season.[web:99][web:100][web:109]

**Infusion (Dried Herb):**  
- **1–5 g dried herb** infused in one cup boiling water, steep 10–15 min; up to **3× daily**.[web:99][web:119]  
- Lower doses for chronic use, higher for short‑term acute cooling.

**Extract:**  
- 1:5 (45% alcohol) — **6–30 ml per day** divided doses.[web:99]  
- Adjust to sensitivity; chickweed is mild but can be surprisingly effective even at modest doses.

**Fluid Extract / Succus:**  
- 1:1 (45%) — **3–15 ml per day**.[web:99][web:119]  
- Fresh plant succus: **3–6 ml per day**, often in short courses.[web:99]

**Topical:**  
- Fresh poultice: crushed fresh herb applied directly for bites, stings, hot rashes.  
- Salve: infused oil + beeswax formulations for chronic eczema/psoriasis itch.[web:99][web:101][web:106]  
- Wash: strong infusion used as a soak or compress.

**Duration:**  
- Safe as food over long periods.  
- Medicinal doses: commonly used for **4–8 weeks**, then reassessed; chronic skin/lymph cases may use lower doses longer.

**Example Protocols**

**Protocol 1: Eczema/Psoriasis Itch Relief (TOPICAL FOUNDATIONAL)**

**Protocol 2: Hot Gastritis / Ulcer Support**

**Protocol 3: Spring Lymph & Skin “Cleanse”**

**Protocol 4: Mild Urinary Irritation**

## KEY BENEFITS & CONTRAINDICATIONS

**Benefits:**  
- Topical and internal **anti‑itch, cooling vulnerary** for hot skin and mucosal conditions.[web:99][web:101][web:106][web:115]  
- **Demulcent/anti‑ulcer** support and GI cooling.[web:115][web:119]  
- Mild **lymph and diuretic** support in damp‑heat states.[web:99][web:103]

**Safety Overview:**  
- Generally **very safe** at typical food/tea doses; main limitations are **allergy, diuretic interactions, and overuse** causing GI upset.[web:99][web:103][web:112][web:117]

**⚠️ CRITICAL CAUTIONS**

- **Pregnancy:**  
  - Some sources say “safe,” others advise **avoiding medicinal doses**; at least one database explicitly says **“do not use medicinally in pregnancy.”**[web:99][web:103][web:117]  
  - For AI app: allow **food‑level use** but **avoid recommending high‑dose medicinal use** in pregnancy unless supervised.

- **Allergy / Caryophyllaceae Sensitivity:**  
  - Rare allergic reactions (rash, itching), especially in those sensitive to carnations/pinks.[web:99][web:103][web:117]

**MAJOR CAUTIONS**

- **Excessive Consumption:**  
  - High saponin load from excessive tea or large therapeutic amounts can cause **nausea, vomiting, diarrhea**.[web:99][web:103][web:117]  
  - Avoid “mega‑infusions” (>6 cups/day) long term.[web:103]

- **Kidney Disorders & Diuretics:**  
  - Mild diuretic action could interact with prescription diuretics, affecting electrolytes (esp. potassium); caution in renal impairment.[web:103]  

- **Children:**  
  - Low‑dose teas (half adult infusion) may be used in older children under pediatric herbal guidance; use cautiously.[web:103]

**Drug Interactions:**  
- Potential additive effects with diuretics and antihypertensives; monitor BP and electrolytes.[web:103]  
- No major CYP or anticoagulant issues documented.

## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Rosa gallica, Rosa centifolia, Rosa damascena and related old garden roses — perennial shrubs native to and cultivated across Europe, Western Asia, and many temperate regions worldwide. Petals for medicine are harvested from unsprayed, fragrant varieties.[web:10][web:20]

**Botanical Family:**  
Rosaceae.[web:10]

**Part Used:**  
Rosae flos — Dried rose petals (sometimes buds), occasionally hips (fruits) for vitamin C and flavonoids; here focus is petals.[web:10]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Flowers harvested in dry morning weather at or just before full bloom, when fragrance and volatile oil content peak. Petals are gently stripped, then dried quickly in shade with good air circulation to preserve color and aroma.[web:10]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Vibrant pink/red color, strongly fragrant; petals flexible rather than brittle; minimal brown/black or mold; intense perfume indicates preserved volatile oils.[web:10][web:20]

**Storage:**  
Stored in airtight, light-protective containers. Best quality within 1–2 years; aroma and color fade gradually.[web:10]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Tea/Infusion:**  
- 1–2 tsp dried petals per 250 ml hot water; steep covered 10–15 minutes; drink 1–3× daily.  
- For emotional support, combine with hawthorn, linden, or lemon balm.[web:10][web:20]

**Cold Infusion:**  
- For preserving volatile oils and a subtler profile, infuse petals in room-temperature water for several hours and drink cool.

**Tincture:**  
- 1:5 (40–60% alcohol) at 1–4 ml 1–3× daily; often blended with other heart herbs.

**Glycerite:**  
- Excellent for children and highly sensitive individuals; 1–4 ml 1–3× daily.

**Topical:**  
- Rose water or diluted hydrosol for facial toner, eye compresses, and energetic clearing of the face/heart field.  

**Honey Preparation:**  
- Petals infused into raw honey for 2–4 weeks; used for sore throat, grief rituals, and as a heart tonic sweetener.[web:20]

**Duration:**

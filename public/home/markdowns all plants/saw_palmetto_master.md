## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Serenoa repens (W. Bartram) Small (Saw Palmetto, Sabal serrulatum) — Small, slow-growing palm native to the southeastern United States (Florida, coastal Carolinas, Gulf Coast). Berries (drupes) are harvested in late summer–autumn when fully ripe (purple-black), then dried and extracted.[web:24][web:28]

**Botanical Family:**  
Arecaceae (palm family).[web:24]

**Part Used:**  
Serenoae fructus — Dried berries (fruits), typically extracted as lipophilic/hexanic or ethanolic extracts for urogenital indications.[web:24][web:34]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Ripe berries collected by hand, dried at low temperatures to preserve fatty acids and sterols, then milled and extracted (often with hexane, ethanol, or supercritical CO₂) to create standardized extracts rich in lipophilic constituents.[web:24][web:26]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Dark purple-black, wrinkled berries; slightly sweet, soapy-aromatic taste; standardized extracts define total fatty acids (~80–90%) and certain marker sterols. Products labeled “lipophilic extract, 160 mg BID” are typical in BPH trials.[web:24][web:25][web:28]

**Storage:**  
Dried berries and extracts stored in airtight containers away from light/heat. Lipid-rich extracts are prone to oxidation—antioxidant-stabilized formulas and opaque packaging preferred.[web:26]

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**  

**Primary Bioactives (Lipophilic Fraction):**  
- Fatty acids (lauric, oleic, linoleic, myristic, palmitic acids): contribute to 5-α-reductase inhibition and anti-androgenic actions on the prostate.[web:26][web:34]  
- Phytosterols (β-sitosterol, stigmasterol, campesterol): anti-inflammatory, modulate cell membrane function in prostate tissue.[web:26]

**Supporting Compounds:**  
- Flavonoids, polysaccharides, and other minor constituents; likely contribute anti-inflammatory and immunomodulatory effects.[web:26]

**Mechanisms:**  
- **5-α-Reductase Modulation:** Inhibits conversion of testosterone to dihydrotestosterone (DHT) in prostate tissue, potentially reducing androgen-driven hyperplasia.[web:26][web:34]  
- **Anti-Inflammatory:** Reduces intraprostatic inflammation (e.g., COX, LOX, inflammatory cytokines), which is linked to progression of BPH and LUTS.[web:34]  
- **Anti-Proliferative & Pro-Apoptotic:** Modulates growth and apoptosis pathways in prostate stromal and epithelial cells.[web:26][web:34]  
- **α-Adrenergic Modulation:** Mild effects on smooth muscle tone in lower urinary tract, improving urine flow.[web:26]

## KEY MECHANISMS

**1. LUTS/BPH Modulation (PRIMARY)**  
Saw palmetto extracts target benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH) and associated lower urinary tract symptoms (LUTS): weak stream, hesitancy, nocturia, urgency.[web:24][web:25][web:28][web:34]  
Effect: Modest symptom improvement (IPSS score reduction, nighttime urination drop, better flow) in some trials, though large RCTs show mixed results vs. placebo.[web:25][web:28]  
Timeline: Several weeks to months; typically 3–6 months for meaningful change.[web:24][web:34]

**2. Anti-Inflammatory / Prostate Tissue Modulation**  
By decreasing intraprostatic inflammation and DHT-driven growth signals, saw palmetto may slow BPH progression.[web:26][web:34]  
Effect: Reduced inflammatory contribution to LUTS and risk of acute urinary retention; mechanistic and observational support.[web:34]

**3. Androgen & Sexual Function Modulation**  
Unlike finasteride, saw palmetto tends to have fewer sexual side effects at typical doses, though data are mixed; some men report stabilized libido/function.[web:25][web:34]

## DOSAGE & PREPARATION

**Standardized Extracts (Most Data):**  
- 320 mg/day of a lipophilic extract, often given as 160 mg twice daily, standardized to ~80–90% fatty acids.[web:24][web:25][web:28][web:34]  

**Crude Berry / Powder:**  
- 1–2 g dried berries 2–3× daily (less standardized; weaker evidence).[web:24]

**Tincture:**  
- 1:5 in 60–70% alcohol: 2–4 ml 2–3× daily; less studied but used in traditional Western herbal practice.

**Duration:**  
- BPH protocols typically 3–6 months before evaluating; may continue long term if tolerated and effective.[web:24][web:34]

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:**  
GRADE B+ — Generally safe, well tolerated, but should always be used under medical evaluation for BPH (to rule out prostate cancer and other serious pathology).[web:24][web:25][web:34]

**Contraindications / Cautions:**  
- Unassessed urinary symptoms (no prior medical evaluation); must rule out malignancy or acute retention risk.[web:24]  
- Pregnancy and lactation: Not relevant for typical user group but avoid use (anti-androgenic activity).[web:24]  
- Hormone-sensitive conditions (male/female): Theoretical anti-androgenic effects warrant caution.

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions:**  
- Theoretical interaction with hormone therapies (androgen-based); limited direct data.  
- Some concerns about interference with PSA tests (masking enlargement) — men using saw palmetto should inform clinicians before PSA testing.[web:24]

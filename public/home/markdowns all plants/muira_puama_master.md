## BOTANICAL & IDENTIFICATION

**Primary Species:**  
Ptychopetalum olacoides Benth. (Muira Puama, “Potency Wood”) — Small Amazonian tree/shrub native to Brazil and neighboring regions; bark and roots traditionally used.[web:61][web:56]

**Botanical Family:**  
Olacaceae.[web:61]

**Part Used:**  
Root and stem bark (dried); sometimes just “bark” in trade.[web:61][web:56]

**Harvesting & Processing:**  
Roots/bark collected from mature plants, cleaned, dried, and cut/sliced; used in decoctions, tinctures, and powders.[web:61][web:56]

**Quality Indicators:**  
Brownish bark/root pieces with slightly aromatic, bitter taste; quality and chemistry vary by region/habitat.[web:61]

**Storage:**  
Dry, airtight, away from heat and light; 2–3 year shelf life typical.

## PHARMACOLOGICAL PROFILE

**Active Compounds:**  

**Key Constituents (partially characterized):**  
- Alkaloids (e.g., muirapuamine — poorly defined structurally in older literature).  
- Terpenes, sterols, fatty acids, essential oils, coumarin-like compounds.[web:61][web:56]

Evidence is still limited; chemistry not as deeply characterized as classical pharmacopeial herbs.

**Mechanisms (Proposed/Partial):**  
- **Central Nervous System Stimulation & Nerve Tonic:** Early studies suggested benefits in nervous system disorders and impotence, possibly via dopaminergic/noradrenergic modulation and increased blood flow.[web:61][web:56]  
- **Libido & Erectile Function:** Two small human trials in France reported improved libido and erectile function in men with sexual dysfunction; mechanisms unclear (likely vascular + CNS).[web:61]  
- **Neuroprotective & Cognitive Support:** Some preclinical data and traditional use suggest improved memory, learning, and stress resilience.[web:56]  
- **Anti-fatigue / Adaptogenic-Like:** Traditional use for fatigue and stamina; may modulate HPA axis and catecholamines.[web:56]

**1. Aphrodisiac & Sexual Function (PRIMARY TRADITIONAL USE)**  
Used for low libido, erectile dysfunction, and decreased sexual satisfaction in both men and women.[web:61][web:56]

## CONTRAINDICATIONS & INTERACTIONS

**Safety Profile:**  
GRADE C+ — Traditional use suggests acceptable safety at moderate doses; modern safety data are limited, so use with caution, especially in cardiovascular and psychiatric patients.

**Contraindications / Cautions:**  
- **Pregnancy & Lactation:** Insufficient safety data; avoid.  
- **Cardiovascular Disease:** As a CNS and possible circulatory stimulant, caution with significant hypertension, arrhythmias, or structural heart disease.  
- **Psychiatric Conditions:** Caution in bipolar, severe anxiety, or agitation due to potential stimulating effects.

**Herb-to-Drug Interactions (Theoretical):**  
- With stimulants or sympathomimetics → additive effects.  
- With antihypertensives → possible antagonism.

The Story

The crimson cap with white veil sits at the base of birch and pine across Sweden's autumn forests, older than every religion that borrowed her imagery. Siberian, Sámi, Baltic and Vedic ceremonial traditions have carried her across millennia — the archetype of the threshold, the fairy-tale mushroom of childhood, the shaman's gateway.

Fresh, her flesh contains both ibotenic acid (an excitatory glutamate analog, unstable) and small amounts of its decarboxylation product muscimol (a GABA-A agonist). The traditional preparation across every culture that worked with her intentionally is the same: slow, patient drying. Warm dehydration converts most of the ibotenic acid into muscimol — softening the compound profile from a chaotic excitatory-depressive toxidrome into a body-anchoring GABAergic nervine.

"Not all gateways are gentle or precise. Some medicines teach by destabilising what you take for granted. True respect includes knowing when not to cross a threshold."

The spagyric extraction takes the slow-dried caps and processes them through the alchemical trinity — ethanol maceration for the tinctures of the plant's soul, calcined ash return for its mineral body, recombined. The result is not "amanita concentrate." It is a threshold key, dosed in single ceremonial micro-drops.

What she carries
Muscimol

GABA-A receptor agonist — same binding site as GABA itself, not the benzodiazepine site. Produces CNS inhibition, muscle relaxation, altered sensory perception, dream-state anchoring. The primary intended active compound after slow-drying.

Muscazone · Muscarine (trace)

Present at low levels in wild specimens. Contribute minor autonomic and sensory notes to the compound profile.

Ibotenic acid (residual)

A glutamate analog and neuroexcitant. Slow-drying converts the majority to muscimol; low residual amounts remain. This is why fresh, un-dried, or un-decarboxylated amanita preparations behave differently — and why source and process matter.

How she's held

Ceremonial micro-dose only. One to three drops under the tongue, in a quiet held container, with time and space free for 4–6 hours after. Onset 30–60 minutes. Effects: body warmth, subtle sensory softening, dream-adjacent inner space, deepened sleep if taken at dusk.

Not for cognitive work. Not for combining with other substances. Not for driving. Not for daily use — this is a threshold ally, not a daily tonic.

Also served in the amuse-bouche of the Fungai Art Dinner Experience, at the same ceremonial micro-dose, held inside a full evening's arc.

Safety · do not skip
  • Never combine with CNS depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids, sleep medications), other GABA-A ligands, or psychoactive medications — additive CNS depression risk.
  • Absolute contraindications: pregnancy, breastfeeding, children and adolescents, seizure disorders, active psychiatric conditions requiring medication.
  • Not a therapy. Fungai Art amanita is offered as a traditional ceremonial preparation, not a medical or psychiatric treatment. If you need therapy or medication, please work with a licensed clinician.
  • Wild, un-dried, or amateur-processed amanita is a poisoning risk. Fatalities documented. This is why we do our own foraging and slow-drying rather than sourcing from anyone else.
  • Start with a single drop the first time. Every body is different.

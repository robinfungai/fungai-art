The Story

Amanita muscaria fruits at the base of birch and pine across Sweden's autumn forests. She has been used ceremonially across Siberian, Sámi, Baltic and Vedic cultures for millennia — framed variously as a sacred ally, a shamanic threshold, a winter-feast bringer of abundance and clarity. Every one of those traditions worked with the mushroom the same way: slow, patient drying.

The traditional drying step is not superstition. It is a decarboxylation reaction. Fresh caps contain ibotenic acid, a glutamate/NMDA-receptor agonist that is neuroexcitatory and can be neurotoxic in higher concentrations. Warm, unhurried drying converts the majority of that ibotenic acid into muscimol — a selective GABA-A receptor agonist that behaves in a completely different way. The two compounds share a molecular backbone but produce opposite CNS actions.

The spagyric extraction takes the slow-dried caps and processes them through the alchemical trinity — ethanol maceration for the plant's soul, calcined ash return for its mineral body, recombined. The intent is not concentration for potency. It is fidelity to a muscimol-primary preparation, held at a sub-perceptual dose so the ally reaches the nervous system without ever taking the driver's seat.

What she carries
Muscimol · the primary carrier

A selective, high-affinity GABA-A receptor agonist — it binds directly at the orthosteric GABA site (not the allosteric benzodiazepine site that anxiolytics act on). Crosses the blood-brain barrier readily; plasma half-life ~90 minutes; excreted largely unchanged in urine, which is why the Siberian "urine cycling" tradition worked pharmacologically. At sub-perceptual micro-doses muscimol produces a quiet limbic dampening, slower cognitive tempo, and improved sleep architecture; at ceremonial doses it produces muscle relaxation, sensory softening, dream-adjacent altered perception. This is the compound the traditional preparation is intended to deliver.

Effects at sub-perceptual micro-dose

Rebalanced sleep pattern — muscimol's GABA-A action supports slow-wave sleep depth and reduces sleep-onset latency at low doses. Cognitive clarity through calm — a quieter over-reactive nervous system tends to read as focus, not sedation, at this dose range. Sacred neuroalchemy — the mushroom's traditional framing as an ally of self-recognition, self-love, and prosperity is echoed in her precise molecular action: not adding stimulation, subtracting noise. Held in a ceremonial container, not a productivity stack.

Ibotenic acid · the pro-drug (brief)

The precursor. Slow-drying decarboxylates the majority of ibotenic acid into muscimol; a small residual remains in every preparation. This is why source and process matter: fresh, un-dried, or amateur-processed amanita is a different (and unsafe) compound profile. Fungai Art foraging and drying is the process the tradition specifies.

How she's held

Sub-perceptual micro-dose: one drop under the tongue in a quiet moment. Effects settle in over 30–60 minutes as a slower nervous system, softer sensory floor, and (if taken at dusk) deeper sleep architecture that same night. This is the daily use band — a cognitive-microdose ally, not an altered state.

Ceremonial dose: two to three drops in a held container with 4–6 hours of open time after. Onset 30–60 minutes. Effects: body warmth, sensory softening, dream-adjacent inner space, slowed cognition. Never combined with other substances. Never for driving.

Safety · do not skip
  • Never combine with CNS depressants (alcohol, benzodiazepines, opioids, sleep medications), other GABA-A ligands, or psychoactive medications — additive CNS depression risk.
  • Absolute contraindications: pregnancy, breastfeeding, children and adolescents, seizure disorders, active psychiatric conditions requiring medication.
  • Not a therapy. Fungai Art amanita is offered as a traditional ceremonial preparation, not a medical or psychiatric treatment. If you need therapy or medication, please work with a licensed clinician.
  • Wild, un-dried, or amateur-processed amanita is a poisoning risk. Fatalities documented. This is why we do our own foraging and slow-drying rather than sourcing from anyone else.
  • Start with a single drop the first time. Every body is different.

The Story

Shilajit is not a plant. It is the exuded resin of the Himalayan mountains themselves — the millions-of-years compression of ancient plant matter into a mineral-rich tar that seeps from rock faces at high altitude in the warm months. The Ayurvedic tradition calls it the destroyer of weakness: a rasayana for the deep tissues, the bones, the immune system, and the reproductive vitality that in that tradition rides on the same current.

The chemistry is unusual. Fulvic acid is a small, negatively-charged molecule that chelates minerals and carries them across cell membranes — it's the transport system that makes the 84 trace minerals in shilajit actually bioavailable. Humic acid supports the microbiome and detoxification. Dibenzo-alpha-pyrones and their chromoproteins are shilajit-specific antioxidants that support mitochondrial CoQ10 activity.

The gold is not decoration. Nanoparticulate gold — colloidal, in the low-nanometer range — has been used in Ayurvedic Swarna Bhasma and European spagyric traditions for centuries as an integrative amplifier: not doing much on its own, but supporting the deep-tissue integration of whatever it is taken with. Cardio-tonic, subtle nervine, and (in the alchemical language) a carrier for the sun's imprint.

"When you take shilajit, you're taking the memory of a mountain."

Not a daily supplement. A ceremonial mineral practice — one pill on a heavy day, before a rite, during recovery, or as part of the shift into a new season. Small, occasional, deep.

What's in the pill
Fulvic acid

The mineral-transporter molecule. Small enough to cross cell membranes; charged enough to carry the trace minerals with it. This is what makes shilajit's 84-element profile actually bioavailable rather than just dietary display.

84 trace minerals

The whole mineral spectrum in trace amounts — iron, magnesium, silica, zinc, chromium, selenium, molybdenum, boron, and dozens more. Not concentrated doses; a full-spectrum ground layer.

Humic acid + dibenzo-α-pyrones

Shilajit-specific antioxidants. Support mitochondrial CoQ10 activity. Documented in human studies for testosterone support, sperm quality, and cognitive protection.

Nanoparticulate gold (Au)

Colloidal gold in the sub-100nm range. The Ayurvedic Swarna Bhasma tradition and European spagyric alchemy both treat gold as an integrative amplifier — subtle cardio-tonic and nervine; primarily a carrier for the depth of whatever it is taken with.

How to take it

One pill, dissolved under the tongue or swallowed with water. Occasional use — not daily. A single pill provides more mineral density than most people need in a week.

Good moments: the first day of a long recovery, the morning of a heavy physical or mental undertaking, the shift between seasons, before or after ceremony, or as a mineral-support marker in the middle of a demanding project. The four-pill format lasts a month or two of intentional use.

Pairs beautifully with Chaga (mineral density + immune depth) and with the Dinner Experience as a pre-arc mineral offering.

Safety notes
  • Sourcing matters. Fungai Art shilajit is purified — the raw resin can contain heavy metals if not properly processed. Lab-tested batches only.
  • Iron overload conditions (hemochromatosis, thalassemia): shilajit provides bioavailable iron. Not appropriate.
  • Kidney disease: discuss with your clinician before use — mineral load and fulvic acid transport can shift electrolyte balance.
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: not recommended — traditional Ayurvedic practice reserves shilajit for the post-partum recovery window rather than pregnancy itself.
  • Gold sensitivity: rare, but if you have a documented gold allergy (from jewellery reactivity), avoid.
  • Not for children.

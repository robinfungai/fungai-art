The Story

Aphrodisiac used to be a real word before it was flattened into greeting-card language. It comes from Aphrodite — the goddess whose domain was not only sex but the whole current of felt aliveness that runs beneath it. Beauty, softness, appetite, the sacredness of the body. Temple Nectar is built for that fuller meaning.

The composition draws from four continents. Saffron from Afghanistan — safranal, the strongest plant antidepressant in the human data. Damiana from Mexico — the traditional Aztec sensual tonic. Catuaba and muira puama from the Amazon — nervous-system aphrodisiacs the Tupi people worked with for centuries. Blue lotus from ancient Egypt — the sacred flower of the temple carvings. Maca negra from the Andes for hormonal ground. Vanilla from the Totonac people of Veracruz to round the whole thing into an actual pleasure to take.

"Sensuality is not a special occasion. It's the baseline the body wants to return to."

Effect: warmth in the chest and belly within 20 minutes. A softening of the armor between you and your own skin. Not stimulation — more like remembering. Works for solo practice, partnered practice, or as a signature ritual for evenings that want to be worth the pause.

The seven ingredients
Saffron Crocus sativus

Safranal and crocin. The strongest human data on any plant antidepressant, meta-analyses comparing favourably to fluoxetine at low dose. Also documented for libido and premenstrual mood. Sourced from Herat highlands in Afghanistan.

Damiana Turnera diffusa

Traditional Aztec sensual tonic. Contains apigenin (GABAergic) and beta-caryophyllene (CB2 activity) — a body-softening, mood-lifting nervine.

Catuaba Erythroxylum catuaba

Amazonian bark tonic. Catuabines A/B/C support dopaminergic signaling and peripheral circulation. Traditional Tupi aphrodisiac for nervous exhaustion and low libido from burnout.

Blue lotus Nymphaea caerulea

Aporphine and nuciferine modulate dopamine and serotonin. Sacred to ancient Egyptian temple practice. Euphoric calm, dream-adjacent softening of perception.

Maca negra Lepidium meyenii

Andean root, black variety specifically (higher macamides than the more common yellow). Supports HPG axis balance, libido and stamina without acting as a hormone itself.

Vanilla Vanilla planifolia

Vanillin and its cousins — documented mood-lift, anxiolytic notes. Also the compound that rounds the rest of the composition into something you actually want to drop under your tongue.

Muira puama Ptychopetalum olacoides

"Potency wood." Amazonian nervous-system aphrodisiac; supports cholinergic and dopaminergic tone. Traditional use for nervous exhaustion, low libido, and general vitality decline.

How to take it

20–40 drops under the tongue, or in a small glass of water. Onset 15–25 minutes; effect settles over an hour and holds for 3–4. Take it when you have time, not before a meeting.

Solo practice: a ritual for reconnecting with the body after long stretches of head-mode. Partnered: shared before an unhurried evening. Cyclic: a few times a week during seasons of low vitality; less often as a signature for particular evenings.

Sometimes paired with the Dinner Experience arc as the palate cleanser before the main sensual courses.

Safety notes
  • Pregnancy and breastfeeding: not recommended — several ingredients (saffron high-dose, blue lotus, damiana) lack safety data or have traditional-use contraindications.
  • SSRIs and MAOIs: saffron has mild serotonergic activity. Compatible for most people at 20–40 drop dose, but discuss with your prescriber if you're on high-dose SSRI or MAOI.
  • Hormone-sensitive conditions: maca's endocrine action is indirect (adaptogenic) but discuss with your clinician if relevant.
  • Alcohol: compatible in moderate wine amounts (this is a ritual composition, not a nightcap booster). Skip if you'll be driving.
  • Blue lotus + heavy CNS depressants: avoid additive sedation.

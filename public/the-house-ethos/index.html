The House Ethos

How we do this.

Six threads that shape every dinner. Not rules — a temperament. And the field-care that holds the container.

i
Fungal devotion.

The kitchen belongs to the mushrooms tonight. Every course carries a fungal note — the ones you already love, the ones you've never heard of, and the ones no restaurant has served you before.

ii
A journey, not a meal.

A dinner is really a series of doorways into the mycelium realm. Each opens a new chamber. You leave the table having tasted a place, not just eaten dinner.

iii
Beyond the usual.

Chanterelle and porcini are welcome — but so are lion's mane, chaga, reishi, morel, cordyceps, black trumpet, and whatever the season offered this week. If you've only met mushrooms sautéed in butter, this is their fuller vocabulary.

iv
Playful reverence.

Serious about the fungi, not about ourselves. There are jokes at this table. The Latin names come out. The plates are beautiful. The evening is meant to delight, not solemnise.

v
The forest at the table.

Autumn foraging arrives on your plate. What can't travel, we compose in the Berlin lab. The room smells of spruce, birch smoke, koji. You're not eating a meal — you're eating a place.

vi
Mycelial connection.

Fungi taught us everything is connected underground. Around this table, the same happens above — strangers become a temporary mycelium for a night, threading conversation, sharing the same breath before the first bite.

Safety · Ethics · Field Care

The container.

Every dinner sits inside a held field. In plain speech, this is what that means.

Allies & dosing

  • Ceremonial micro-amount only. Sacred allies dosed in food, never over.
  • Each ally named at the table — effects and purpose explained.
  • Full allergen and ingredient disclosure before the meal.
  • No pressure to eat any course. Abstention fully respected.

Table & field care

  • Sovereign guests, closed field. Shared agreements at the start.
  • Pace held for tasting, listening, integration between courses.
  • Breath together at the close.
  • This is a dinner — not therapy, not medical treatment.

Ecological respect

  • Partners, not commodities. Every ingredient sustainably sourced or self-foraged.
  • Strict seasonal sourcing. Menus shift with the year.
  • Never harvesting from vulnerable ecosystems.
  • What we take, we take with permission.

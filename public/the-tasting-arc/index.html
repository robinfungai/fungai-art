The Tasting Arc

A series of movements,
one long evening.

Every Fungai Art dinner unfolds as an arc — a shape held from the first drop to the last sip of tea. Here is how the evening moves.

I
Movement I
The aperitif.

A mycofermented shot on arrival — chaga, koji, citrus, ginger — alongside a pour from the New Tyme Tonics elixir line. Opens the palate, warms the gut, sets the pace. Non-alcoholic base; natural-wine pairing on request.

II
Movement II
The amuse-bouche.

A ceremonial micro-drop of Fungai Art amanita tincture on a spelt-and-birch-bark cracker, pine pollen dust. Introduced at the table with full clarity of dose and effect. Optional — refusable without comment.

III
Movement III
Broths & ferments.

Slow-cooked mushroom broths, koji-cured vegetables, foraged pickles. The bitter and umami foundations that carry the rest of the meal. A twelve-hour chaga-cordyceps-porcini broth is the umami spine most nights.

IV
Movements IV–VI
The main movements.

Three savoury courses built around foraged fungi of the season, cordyceps, chaga, pine pollen, chilcuague. Koji-cured beetroot with lingonberry oil. Wild mushroom with juniper and hazelnut miso. Aged rye with cultured butter and reindeer-moss salt.

V
Movement VII
The bittersweet close.

Cacao and adaptogen dessert — cocoa cream with reishi's herbal note, candied pine needles, flake salt — followed by warm schisandra-rosehip tea. Integration built into the arc. The meal doesn't just end, it settles.

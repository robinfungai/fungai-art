# Fungai Art extraction methods

How we extract, in house. Ratios and timings here are our working
practice, not a published standard — MYCO cites this as our own method.

Seeded from the method list MYCO already carried in its system prompt
(`netlify/functions/myco-agent.js`), so nothing was lost when the
knowledge moved here. Correct and extend it as practice changes.

## Spagyric extraction

The Paracelsan method: separate, purify, recombine. Three principles —
sulfur (the volatile oils), mercury (the alcohol-soluble spirit), salt
(the mineral ash of the calcined plant body). The salts are returned to
the tincture, which is what separates a spagyric from a plain tincture.

Full arc runs 6–10 weeks. Used for our signature elixirs where the
mineral return matters to the finished character of the extract.

## Cold extract (maceration)

40–60% ethanol maceration, 2–6 weeks.

- Fresh plant: 1:3 plant-to-solvent
- Dried plant: 1:5

The default for aromatic and alkaloid-rich material where heat would
cost us the volatiles.

## Decoction

Long simmer, for roots, barks and woody mushrooms.

- 1:20 plant to water
- 45–90 minutes

This is the water-soluble half of a double extraction, and stands alone
for mineral-rich root work.

## Double extraction

Hot water for the beta-glucans, alcohol for the triterpenes, then the
two are combined. Essential for Reishi, Chaga and Turkey Tail — a single
solvent misses half of what those fungi carry.

## Oleoresin

Fat-soluble constituents drawn into a lipid carrier.

- 60 °C, 4–8 hours
- 1:8 plant to oil

Used where the actives will not travel in water or ethanol.

## Choosing a method

Match the solvent to the constituent, not to the plant:

- Volatile oils and resins → alcohol, cold, or oleoresin
- Beta-glucans and polysaccharides → hot water
- Triterpenes → alcohol
- Minerals → calcination and return (spagyric), or long decoction
- Both polysaccharides and triterpenes in one plant → double extraction
